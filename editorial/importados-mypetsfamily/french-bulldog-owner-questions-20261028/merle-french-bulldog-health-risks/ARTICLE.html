What does merle mean in a French Bulldog?

Merle is a pigmentation pattern that may create irregular diluted patches. It is linked to variants in a region of the PMEL gene, and visible patterns can differ even when dogs carry merle-associated variants.

'Blue merle,' 'lilac merle,' and similar labels combine color marketing language with genetic concepts. The exact label on a listing is not a genetic test result.

Are all merle French Bulldogs deaf or blind?

No. Having one merle-associated variant does not mean a dog is deaf or blind. The most serious documented concerns involve particular double-merle combinations, which can be associated with auditory and ocular abnormalities.

Other causes of impaired vision or hearing also exist. Owners should not diagnose a dog from eye color, coat color, or a breeder's adjective.

Why is double merle different?

Two merle-associated alleles can be inherited when both parents carry merle variants. UC Davis explains that double-merle offspring can have a greater risk of auditory and ophthalmic problems, sometimes accompanied by extensive white areas.

The genetics are more complex than the simple slogan 'one gene versus two.' Variant length and cryptic patterns complicate visual prediction. Laboratory testing is important when interpreting breeding risks.

How can hearing and eyesight be checked?

If a dog appears not to respond to sound, arrange a veterinary examination; brainstem auditory evoked response (BAER) testing can assess hearing, including one-sided deafness. An ophthalmologist can examine structural eye concerns.

Do not rely on clapping behind a puppy as a definitive hearing test. Vibration, movement, and the other ear can mask a hearing deficit.

What should owners ask before breeding?

Request documented merle genetic testing and specialist guidance. Avoid assuming that two dogs are safe to mate merely because one has a solid-looking coat; some merle variants are visually subtle.

Breed decisions should also account for French Bulldog respiratory and orthopedic health, not treat coat genetics as the only welfare concern.
