A dry French Bulldog nose is usually harmless, but a thick, crusty or cracked nose may be nasal hyperkeratosis. Here is how to tell the difference and what helps.

Key takeaways

  • A warm or dry nose on its own is not a reliable sign that a dog is sick.
  • Nasal hyperkeratosis is a buildup of thick, crusty keratin on the nose. In a 2025 study of 20 affected dogs, 12 were French Bulldogs.[1]
  • A twice-daily emollient balm reduced symptoms by more than half in that small pilot study.[1]
  • Sores, bleeding, loss of pigment or changes in the nose's texture need a vet to rule out other conditions.

Is a dry nose a sign of illness?

Not by itself. A dog's nose changes moisture through the day. It is often dry after sleep, in dry or heated indoor air, after sun exposure, or simply because the dog has not licked it recently. Judge your dog's health by appetite, energy, drinking, breathing and bathroom habits, not by how the nose feels.

French Bulldogs have an extra reason for a dry nose: with a short face, some may not reach the top of the nose easily with their tongue to keep it moist.

Nasal hyperkeratosis in French Bulldogs

Hyperkeratosis means the skin makes too much keratin, the tough protein in the outer layer of skin. On the nose, it shows up as:

  • Thick, rough, crusty skin, often on the top edge of the nose
  • Hard, feathery or horn-like growths
  • Dry cracks that can become sore or bleed
  • Sometimes similar thickening on the paw pads

Flat-faced breeds seem especially prone. In a 2025 study of dogs with nasal hyperkeratosis, "brachycephalic breeds were overrepresented, with French Bulldogs being the most frequent" (12 of 20 dogs).[1] The condition is usually managed rather than cured.

In that study, owners applied an emollient balm twice daily for 60 days. Symptom scores dropped by about 55% according to the investigators and about 62% according to owners, with no side effects reported.[1] The authors call it a pilot study without a control group, so results "should be considered preliminary."[1]

Other causes of a dry or cracked nose

  • Sunburn, especially on pink or light-colored noses
  • Allergies, which are common in French Bulldogs. A UK study found about 7.7 times the odds of allergic skin disease compared with other dogs.[2]
  • Dehydration, usually together with other signs like lethargy and dry gums
  • Autoimmune skin diseases, which can cause loss of pigment, sores or a smooth, shiny nose surface
  • Infections

Safe home care

  • Ask your vet to recommend a dog-safe nose balm. Many contain shea butter, plant oils or ceramides.
  • Apply a thin layer after meals or walks, then distract your dog with a toy for a few minutes so it absorbs.
  • Avoid human products with zinc oxide, tea tree oil, salicylic acid, menthol or fragrance. Dogs lick their noses, so ingredients get swallowed.
  • Do not pick or peel crusts. This can cause bleeding and infection.
  • Use a humidifier in dry, heated rooms in winter.
  • Provide shade and limit midday sun, which also protects your Frenchie from heat.

When to see a vet

Book a visit if you notice:

  • Cracks, sores or bleeding
  • Loss of color on the nose, or a nose that changes from rough to smooth and shiny
  • Crusting that spreads to the lips, eyes or paws
  • Discharge from the nose, sneezing or nosebleeds
  • A dry nose together with lethargy, poor appetite or breathing changes

Take photos over a few weeks. They help your vet see how the nose is changing.
