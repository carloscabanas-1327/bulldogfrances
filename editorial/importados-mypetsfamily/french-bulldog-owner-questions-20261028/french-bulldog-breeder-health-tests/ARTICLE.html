Which tests are recommended for French Bulldogs?

The French Bull Dog Club of America's health registry information includes hip, eye, patella, and cardiac evaluations for the breed's CHIC framework. The club also discusses additional tests and respiratory-function grading.

Do not confuse a test for a coat color trait with screening for breathing difficulty, joint disease, or an eye disorder.

What does a CHIC number prove?

A CHIC number means the required breed-specific tests were recorded and made publicly available under the program rules. The Orthopedic Foundation for Animals explicitly notes that the number does not mean every result was normal.

Read each actual test result and its date. Ask what was checked, who performed the examination, and whether any abnormal findings affected breeding choices.

How do you assess breathing?

Ask whether the parents received an appropriate functional respiratory assessment and what the grades mean. The Cambridge system assesses breathing clinically, including after controlled exercise.

A dog can have an attractive face and still have obstructive airway disease. Avoid interpreting a nostril photograph or a short video as formal respiratory screening.

What else should a responsible breeder explain?

Expect a transparent conversation about birth history, veterinary care, the mother's welfare, early socialization, relevant hereditary problems, and what happens if a puppy develops a problem.

A breeder should allow time for questions and should not use urgency or an unusually rare color as a substitute for health evidence.

Which records should you keep?

Save the parents' health reports, the puppy's veterinary records, identification information, and any written agreement. Ask the breeder to explain unfamiliar abbreviations.

If records are missing, inconsistent, or impossible to verify, pause the decision. Walking away is reasonable when welfare information cannot be obtained.
