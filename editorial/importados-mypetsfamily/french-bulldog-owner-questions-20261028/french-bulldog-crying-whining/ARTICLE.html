Why does my French Bulldog whine or cry? Changes in vocalization can reflect discomfort, anxiety, unmet needs or respiratory noise requiring veterinary attention.

Key takeaways

  • Whining may signal needs, arousal, discomfort or distress. The sound alone cannot identify a cause.
  • New or unusual crying, especially when moving, can mean pain. Back problems such as disc disease are one possible cause.[1]
  • Some "crying" sounds are actually breathing noise. Learn to tell them apart.[2]
  • After ruling out pain and distress, reward calm alternative behavior instead of punishing vocalization. Veterinary behaviorists recommend reward-based methods.[3]

Common reasons Frenchies whine

  • Wanting attention or play. Frenchies were bred as companion dogs and often love being close to people.
  • Needing the bathroom. Especially puppies and seniors.
  • Hunger or excitement around mealtimes and walks.
  • Boredom. Not enough gentle exercise, play or puzzle toys.
  • Anxiety. New places, loud noises, visitors or being left alone.
  • Discomfort. Too hot, too cold, an itchy spot, a full bladder.

Crying that may mean pain

Call your vet if crying is new, sudden or comes with:

  • Yelping when picked up, touched on the back, or when turning the head. Cornell notes dogs with disc problems may "avoid turning their head in one direction" and walk with "a wobbly gait."[1]
  • Reluctance to climb stairs, jump or walk
  • Shaking ears, scratching or licking one spot
  • Straining to pee or poop
  • Hiding, restlessness or panting at rest

Emergency: sudden hind-leg weakness, dragging the legs or inability to stand requires immediate veterinary assessment. Do not manipulate a painful spine or force the dog to walk.[1]

Is it crying or a breathing sound?

Breathing sounds may be mistaken for whining. Cambridge describes respiratory noise and sleep-disordered breathing among possible signs of BOAS, but other airway problems can also cause unusual sounds.[2] If the sound happens with each breath, gets worse with exercise or heat, or happens when your dog is asleep, mention it to your vet and take a short video.

Puppy crying at night

Some puppies vocalize during their first nights in a new home, but persistent crying should not automatically be dismissed as normal: first check toileting, discomfort, temperature and signs of distress.

  • Put the crate next to your bed for the first weeks so the puppy can hear and smell you.
  • Take the puppy out to potty right before bed and once during the night if needed.
  • Add a soft blanket and a safe chew toy.
  • Keep night trips boring: quick potty, back to bed, no play.
  • Make the crate a happy place during the day with treats and meals.

Crying when left alone

  • Practice short departures, a few seconds then a few minutes, and build up slowly.
  • Offer a safely tested food puzzle only if the dog can use it calmly; severely distressed dogs may refuse food and need veterinary behavior support.
  • Keep arrivals and departures calm.
  • A camera can show whether your dog settles or stays distressed.
  • If your dog howls, drools, destroys things or has accidents when alone, talk to your vet. ASPCA explains that distress has several mimics and that severe cases often need a supervised behavior plan, sometimes with medication.[4]

How to reduce attention whining

  1. Meet the need first: potty, water, food, exercise, comfort.
  2. Reinforce relaxed behavior when the dog's needs are met, without waiting out fear, pain or respiratory difficulty.
  3. Do not ignore signs of distress. For established attention-seeking behavior, reward a calm alternative. Never withhold comfort or medical help because a dog is vocalizing.
  4. Never punish crying. AVSAB recommends "only reward-based training methods" for all dog training, including behavior problems.[3]
  5. Add enrichment: sniff walks, short training sessions, chew toys.
