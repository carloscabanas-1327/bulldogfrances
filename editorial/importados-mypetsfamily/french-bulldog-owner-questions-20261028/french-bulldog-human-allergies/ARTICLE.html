Are French Bulldogs hypoallergenic?

No. The AAAAI is direct about this: "Contrary to popular opinion, there are no truly 'hypoallergenic breeds' of dogs or cats. Allergic dander in cats and dogs is not affected by length of hair or fur, nor by the amount of shedding."[1]

Some people react less to certain individual dogs than to others, but that varies person to person and dog to dog. A breed label cannot promise it. Be cautious of any seller who markets French Bulldogs, or "fluffy" Frenchies, as hypoallergenic.

What actually causes dog allergies

According to the AAAAI, "the proteins found in a pet's dander, skin flakes, saliva and urine can cause an allergic reaction or aggravate asthma symptoms in some people."[1] These proteins are tiny. They stick to furniture, clothing and walls, and can stay in a home long after a dog has left.

Symptoms can include sneezing, a runny or stuffy nose, itchy eyes, coughing, wheezing, hives or skin rashes where a dog has licked you.

Frenchie traits that matter for allergies

  • Short coat, but they shed. Short hairs still carry dander and spread through the house.
  • Drooling and licking. Many Frenchies drool, especially those with breathing problems, and saliva is a source of allergens.
  • Close contact. Frenchies are lap dogs that like to sleep with their people, which increases exposure.
  • Skin problems. Frenchies are prone to allergic skin disease themselves. Flaky, inflamed skin may shed more dander.

How to test before you commit

  1. See an allergist. Testing can confirm whether you are allergic to dogs and how strongly.
  2. Spend several hours with French Bulldogs on more than one occasion, indoors, including petting and close contact.
  3. Consider fostering a Frenchie through a rescue for a few weeks.
  4. Involve everyone in the household, especially anyone with asthma.

Rehoming a dog because of allergies is hard on both the dog and the family, so testing first is worth the effort.

Living with a Frenchie when you have allergies

  • Keep the dog out of the bedroom.
  • Use a HEPA air purifier and vacuum with a HEPA filter.
  • Choose hard floors and washable covers over carpets and heavy fabric.
  • Wash your hands after handling the dog, and avoid face licking.
  • Bathe the dog on a schedule your vet approves. Overbathing can irritate Frenchie skin.
  • Ask your allergist about medicines or allergy shots (immunotherapy).
