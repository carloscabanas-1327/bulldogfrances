What causes dog allergies?

Allergies are reactions to proteins found in skin flakes, saliva and urine, not simply to the visible strands of hair.

Fur can carry allergens and outdoor particles, but a short coat cannot make a dog allergen-free.

Would a fluffy or low-shedding Frenchie be safer?

No French Bulldog coat type guarantees that an allergic person will tolerate the dog. Longer or shorter hair does not remove allergenic proteins.

Individual reactions vary, so breed descriptions are poor substitutes for medical allergy assessment.

How can a family assess the problem?

Consult an allergist to identify triggers, especially when asthma or significant breathing symptoms are involved.

Do not diagnose a child's allergies from an online list of supposedly hypoallergenic breeds.

What if someone already lives with a Frenchie?

Discuss household cleaning, filtration and symptom management with the relevant medical specialist. Changes may reduce exposure but do not promise complete relief.

Consider both human respiratory health and the dog's welfare when choosing an ongoing plan.

Does the dog's color matter?

There is no credible basis for claiming that merle, blue, black, white, or cream French Bulldogs are allergy-free because of their color.

Separate coat genetics from human allergic responses and avoid marketing claims that blur the distinction.
