Caring for a pregnant French Bulldog takes extra planning. Most Frenchies need veterinary help to give birth, so preparation starts on day one.

Key takeaways

  • Pregnancy in dogs lasts about 62 to 64 days.[1]
  • French Bulldogs had about 9 times the odds of dystocia (difficult birth) compared with other dogs in a large UK study.[2]
  • Plan the birth with your vet early, including whether a scheduled cesarean section is safer.
  • Pregnancy adds strain on breathing and heat tolerance in a flat-faced breed.[3]

Confirming the pregnancy

Pregnancy in dogs lasts about 62 to 64 days.[1] Your vet can confirm it with:

  • Ultrasound: usually from about 3 to 4 weeks after mating
  • Blood test (relaxin): from around the same time
  • X-ray: in the last week or two, to count puppies and check their size. This is very useful for planning a Frenchie delivery.

Week-by-week care

WeeksCare
1 to 4Normal diet and gentle walks. Ask your vet about deworming, parasite control and which medicines to avoid.
5 to 6Begin moving to a puppy or growth formula as your vet advises. Feed smaller, more frequent meals.
7 to 8X-ray to count puppies. Set up a quiet whelping area. Confirm your delivery plan and emergency clinic.
9Short, calm walks only. Keep her cool. Many vets track her temperature in the final days.

Keep her lean and comfortable. Do not give supplements such as extra calcium unless your vet prescribes them.

Why Frenchie births are high-risk

A Royal Veterinary College study found French Bulldogs had about 9 times the odds of dystocia, or difficult birth, compared with other dogs.[2] The main reason is a mismatch: puppies have large heads and broad shoulders, while the mother's birth canal is narrow.

Breathing is a second concern. Labor is hard physical work, and the breed is prone to BOAS, which causes noisy breathing and heat intolerance.[3] Anesthesia for a cesarean also needs a team experienced with flat-faced dogs.

Planning the delivery

  • Ask your vet whether they recommend a planned cesarean section, and how they will time it.
  • Find out what happens if labor starts at night or on a weekend. Save the emergency clinic number.
  • Prepare a warm, clean whelping box in a quiet room.
  • Have puppy supplies ready: towels, a heat source with a safe area to move away from it, a scale and a notebook.

Emergency signs

Call your vet or emergency clinic right away if she:

  • Strains strongly for 20 to 30 minutes without delivering a puppy
  • Has green or black discharge before the first puppy
  • Rests more than 2 hours between puppies when more are expected
  • Goes past her due date without signs of labor
  • Has breathing trouble, collapses or seems very weak

Ask your vet to give you their own thresholds in advance, since they may differ for a planned cesarean.

After the puppies arrive

  • Make sure every puppy nurses within the first hours.
  • Weigh puppies daily. They should gain weight steadily.
  • Check the mother's incision (after a cesarean), mammary glands and discharge every day.
  • Watch for restlessness, trembling or stiffness in the mother, which can signal low calcium (eclampsia), an emergency.
  • Keep the room warm but not hot, for the mother's breathing.
