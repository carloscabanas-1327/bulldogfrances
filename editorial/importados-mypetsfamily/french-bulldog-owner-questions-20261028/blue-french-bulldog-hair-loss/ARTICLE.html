Is your blue French Bulldog losing hair? A genetic condition called color dilution alopecia is one possible cause, but not the only one.

Key takeaways

  • Blue (and lilac) coats come from a recessive variant of the MLPH gene that dilutes pigment.[1]
  • Some dilute dogs develop color dilution alopecia (CDA): thinning hair and skin problems in the blue areas, usually starting before 1 year of age.[1]
  • CDA is managed, not cured. Merck describes it as cosmetic, not a threat to overall health.[1]
  • Allergies, mites and infections also cause hair loss and need different treatment, so see a vet for a diagnosis.

What makes a French Bulldog blue

A "blue" French Bulldog is genetically a black dog with diluted pigment. The dilution comes from a variant in the melanophilin (MLPH) gene, inherited in an autosomal recessive way. According to the Merck Veterinary Manual, this variant "renders black genotypes blue and liver genotypes beige or fawn."[1] A puppy must inherit the variant from both parents to be dilute.

The current AKC standard (2018) accepts white, cream and fawn, with markings such as brindle and piebald, and lists "blue" and "blue fawn" among its disqualifying colors.[2] The French Bulldog Rescue Network counts blue among the "fad colors" whose popularity is recent, warning that "we do not yet know what health problems might be associated with each new color."[3]

What color dilution alopecia looks like

Merck explains that dogs with CDA "are born with normal hair coats; however, before they are 1 year old, they begin to develop progressive folliculitis and hypotrichosis confined to blue- or fawn-colored areas."[1] In plain terms:

  • Hair becomes dull, dry and brittle, then thins out
  • Patches of thin or bald skin appear, often on the back and sides first
  • Skin becomes flaky and scaly
  • Small bumps or pimples appear from infected hair follicles
  • Areas of white hair are usually spared

Not every blue dog develops CDA. Merck notes the condition is most widely recognized in Doberman Pinschers and is common in several other dilute breeds.[1] How often it affects blue French Bulldogs has not been well studied.

Other causes of hair loss

Hair loss in a blue Frenchie is not automatically CDA. French Bulldogs are prone to several skin problems, and many are treatable:

  • Allergies (environmental or food): usually itchy, often affecting paws, face, belly and ears.
  • Mites such as demodex: patchy hair loss, sometimes around the eyes and face.
  • Bacterial or yeast infections: odor, redness, greasy skin.
  • Hormonal conditions such as low thyroid function.
  • Skin fold infections: a UK study found French Bulldogs had about 11 times the odds of skin fold dermatitis compared with other dogs.[4]

How a vet diagnoses it

Your vet will usually start by ruling out the treatable causes with skin scrapings, tape tests, cytology and sometimes blood tests. If CDA is suspected, a skin biopsy confirms the diagnosis.[1] A DNA test for the dilute gene shows whether a dog carries dilution, but it cannot tell you whether that dog will develop CDA.

Caring for a dog with CDA

Merck states that "congenital or genetic causes of hair loss are managed but not cured," and that care focuses on controlling dry skin, scaling and superficial infections.[1] Your vet may suggest:

  • Gentle medicated shampoos on a regular schedule
  • Moisturizing sprays or rinses
  • Antibiotics or antiseptic treatment for flare-ups of infection
  • Omega-3 fatty acid supplements, if your vet recommends them
  • Sun protection for bald skin: shade, a light T-shirt, or a pet-safe sunscreen your vet approves
  • Soft brushes only; avoid harsh grooming

The good news: Merck notes this disorder "is cosmetic and will not affect the dog's overall health."[1]

Before buying a blue puppy

  • Ask whether the parents or earlier puppies have had hair loss or chronic skin problems.
  • Ask for the standard health tests (hips, eyes, patellas, heart) and a breathing grade, not just color DNA.
  • Be cautious when a premium price is based on color. Color tells you nothing about the dog's breathing, spine or overall health.
