Why does a French Bulldog grow long hair?

Several recessive variants of the FGF5 gene are associated with longer coats in dogs. UC Davis specifically lists an FGF5 variant found in French Bulldogs and explains that the longhair trait is often called 'fluffy.'

A long coat is a genetic trait, not proof that a dog is a different breed or a healthier version of the French Bulldog.

Does fluffy fur cause respiratory disease?

The inherited coat-length trait is distinct from BOAS, the upper-airway syndrome associated with brachycephalic anatomy. There is no sound basis for saying that all fluffy Frenchies breathe worse simply because they have longer hair.

Equally, the word 'fluffy' does not offer protection from airway disease. Evaluate the individual dog's respiratory function and body condition.

What care changes with a longer coat?

Longer fur may need more frequent gentle brushing, particularly where hair rubs or tangles. Inspect the skin for redness, discomfort, excessive scratching, or moisture trapped in folds.

Choose grooming methods that protect the skin. Shaving or aggressive washing without a specific reason may not address the underlying problem.

What if the fluffy coat starts thinning?

Patchy bald spots, odor, soreness, or persistent itching deserve veterinary assessment. Parasites, allergies, infection, and hormonal disorders can all lead to coat problems.

Avoid diagnosing coat disease from its length or color. A veterinarian may recommend skin examination and tests based on the distribution and duration of the changes.

What should a breeder disclose?

A responsible discussion covers respiratory screening, hips, patellas, eyes, cardiac examinations, and relevant genetic testing. Coat length should never eclipse functional health.

Ask for original dated records rather than assurances that a 'rare' coat makes the dog exceptional.
