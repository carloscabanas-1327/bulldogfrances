Is your Frenchie in heat for the first time? Here is how long a heat cycle lasts, what to expect, and how to keep her safe and comfortable.

Key takeaways

  • Female dogs usually come into heat about twice a year, with an average of 7 months between cycles.[1]
  • The bleeding stage (proestrus) lasts about 9 days, followed by the fertile stage (estrus), also about 9 days on average.[1]
  • She can get pregnant during heat even if bleeding has slowed. Keep her away from intact males for the whole cycle.
  • French Bulldogs had about 9 times the odds of difficult births compared with other dogs, so an accidental pregnancy is a real risk.[2]

When will my Frenchie have her first heat?

Timing varies widely between individual dogs. Small and medium breeds often have their first heat sometime during their first year, but some are earlier or later. Ask your vet what to expect for your dog, and note the date when it happens. After the first cycle, Merck notes the interval between cycles "can range from 4 to 13 months," with an average of 7 months.[1]

The stages of a heat cycle

StageTypical lengthWhat happens
ProestrusAbout 9 days (2 days to 3 weeks)Swollen vulva, bloody discharge. Males are attracted, but she usually refuses them.
EstrusAbout 9 days (3 days to 3 weeks)Fertile period. Discharge often turns lighter. She may accept a male.
DiestrusAbout 2 monthsHormone levels stay high whether or not she is pregnant.
AnestrusSeveral monthsResting phase until the next cycle.

Proestrus and estrus lengths are from the Merck Veterinary Manual.[1]

Signs she is in heat

  • Swollen vulva
  • Bloody or pink discharge (some dogs keep themselves very clean, so it can be easy to miss)
  • Licking the genital area more often
  • Peeing more often or marking
  • Restlessness, clinginess or mood changes
  • Male dogs suddenly very interested in her

Caring for her during heat

  • Supervise every moment outside. Never leave her alone in a yard. Males can jump or dig under fences.
  • Walk on a leash only, and avoid dog parks.
  • Use washable dog diapers or heat pants indoors if you like. Remove them for potty breaks and keep the area clean and dry.
  • Offer comfort and calm. Extra cuddles, chew toys and quiet time help.
  • Keep exercise gentle, as always for a Frenchie.

If an accidental mating happens, call your vet promptly to discuss options. Because French Bulldogs often need cesarean sections, an unplanned pregnancy carries real risk.[2]

Warning signs after heat

Call your vet promptly if, in the weeks after a heat, she:

  • Drinks and pees much more than usual
  • Has pus-like or foul-smelling discharge
  • Seems tired, vomits or loses her appetite
  • Has a swollen belly

These can be signs of pyometra, a serious uterine infection in unspayed females that needs urgent treatment.

Spaying: questions to ask your vet

The best age to spay depends on your dog's size, health and lifestyle, and guidance has changed over time. Ask your vet:

  • "What age do you recommend for spaying a French Bulldog, and why?"
  • "Should we wait until after her first heat?"
  • "How do you manage anesthesia in a flat-faced breed?"
  • "Can her airway be checked, or treated, during the same anesthesia?"
