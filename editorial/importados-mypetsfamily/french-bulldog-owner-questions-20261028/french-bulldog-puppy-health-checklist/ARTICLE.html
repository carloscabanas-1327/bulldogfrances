What should you check before bringing a French Bulldog puppy home? Start with breathing, movement, eyes, and health records.

What should breathing sound like?

A puppy should not be struggling to draw breath during gentle normal activity. Marked effort, blue or gray gums, collapse, or unusual distress requires immediate veterinary care.

Some young dogs with significant anatomical issues may appear comfortable at rest. Breathing sounds alone cannot replace a clinical airway assessment.

What should you notice about eyes and skin?

Look for obvious redness, persistent discharge, squinting, open sores, or irritated skin folds. These findings need medical interpretation rather than an assumption that they are normal for the breed.

Puppies with painful eyes should be examined promptly. Do not use leftover eye drops or topical treatments without veterinary direction.

How do movement and body shape matter?

Observe the puppy walking freely on a safe surface. Persistent limping, obvious discomfort, or inability to move normally should prompt further evaluation.

A brief visit cannot rule out hip, spinal, or patellar disease. Parent health records and a veterinary examination provide different types of information.

What questions should you ask about upbringing?

Ask where the litter was raised, how the puppies were handled, what they were fed, and what exposure they have had to ordinary household sounds and experiences.

A good answer emphasizes gradual and humane socialization rather than promising that every puppy is already perfectly trained.

What should happen before a final decision?

Review the individual puppy's records and the parents' documented health tests. Ask about any breathing assessment and the breeder's plan if a hereditary condition appears later.

If you feel rushed, unable to see relevant welfare information, or pressured toward a particular color, step away until the unanswered questions are resolved.
