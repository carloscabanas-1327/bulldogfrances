What changes are part of aging?

Older dogs may slow down, sleep differently, or develop changes in vision and hearing. However, marked lethargy, pain and sudden weakness are not signs to simply accept.

Keep a dated log of changes in appetite, energy, mobility and breathing so subtle trends are not missed.

How often should the vet examine a senior dog?

AAHA guidance supports regular preventive assessment with closer follow-up for senior pets, tailored to medical conditions and individual risk.

Ask about dental health, body and muscle condition, medication review, mobility, and any indicated laboratory screening.

What makes a home safer?

Non-slip rugs, accessible resting places, and ramps where practical can reduce unnecessary slips and jumps. Arrange food and water where movement is comfortable.

Changes should match the dog's actual needs; sudden mobility loss or pain deserves medical evaluation.

How does breathing affect activity?

BOAS can make exertion and heat more difficult at any age. A slower older dog may have joint pain, airway disease, heart problems or more than one concern.

Do not assume every breathless episode is simply caused by being old.

Which changes need quick medical attention?

Collapse, sudden neurologic weakness, severe breathing difficulty, acute eye pain, or inability to urinate require urgent assessment.

A good senior care plan emphasizes comfort, meaningful activity and prompt response to treatable problems.
