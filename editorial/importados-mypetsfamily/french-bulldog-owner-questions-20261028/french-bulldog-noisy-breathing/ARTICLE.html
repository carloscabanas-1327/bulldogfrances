Is snorting normal for a French Bulldog?

Noisy breathing is common enough in short-faced dogs that some people normalize it, but common does not mean harmless. The airway may be restricted by narrow nostrils or soft tissues within the nose and throat.

Not every occasional sound indicates severe BOAS. The pattern, intensity, and effect on the dog's daily function are important.

What changes matter most?

Worsening noise, unusual abdominal effort, difficulty recovering after activity, restless sleep, or regurgitation alongside breathing trouble all deserve discussion with a veterinarian.

Sudden severe distress, blue gums, or collapse requires emergency assessment. Keep the dog calm and cool while seeking help; do not force food, water, or exercise.

Can you judge breathing from a video?

A short recording can be useful to show your vet, particularly when symptoms occur only at home. But it cannot show the full airway anatomy or replace examination.

The University of Cambridge uses a structured clinical exercise assessment and respiratory grading, with professional supervision.

How does body weight affect breathing?

Excess body weight can increase the burden on already vulnerable airways. Ask your vet for an individualized feeding and weight-management plan rather than abruptly restricting meals.

Avoid vigorous exercise or hot weather testing to see whether a dog can keep up with other breeds.

What should an owner do next?

Arrange assessment before symptoms become a crisis. Ask whether your dog would benefit from formal BOAS grading, weight management, or referral to a clinician experienced in brachycephalic airway care.

A plan based on function is more useful than labeling a dog 'healthy' simply because its breathing sounds familiar.
