A French Bulldog with sudden back pain or weak legs may need urgent veterinary care. Learn which signs matter most.

Which signs are urgent?

Sudden inability to walk, dragging legs, falling, severe neck or back pain, or changes in bladder control require urgent veterinary assessment.

Do not encourage a weak or painful dog to jump, climb stairs, or walk to 'work it off.'

Could a spinal disc be involved?

Intervertebral disc disease (IVDD) can cause neck or back pain, weakness, coordination problems, and sometimes paralysis. Other orthopedic and neurologic problems can look similar.

Only a veterinarian can determine whether the signs reflect disc disease, injury, joint pain, or another condition.

What should you do before reaching a vet?

Minimize unnecessary movement, keep the dog safely supported, and contact the clinic for transport advice. Avoid human pain relievers.

Do not manipulate the spine or attempt stretching exercises. A dog in pain may bite even when normally gentle.

How is a diagnosis made?

A veterinarian will assess neurologic function, pain and medical history. Imaging, sometimes including MRI or CT, may be needed when a spinal lesion is suspected.

Ordinary X-rays do not show every cause of spinal cord compression, so the diagnostic plan depends on findings.

Can risk be reduced?

Keeping a healthy body condition, reducing repeated high-impact jumps, and addressing pain early may help manage mechanical stress, though no approach eliminates every inherited or structural risk.

A safe home setup with ramps and non-slip surfaces may be especially useful for dogs that already have mobility concerns.
