## Genetics: pied / white / deafness
- Piebald caused by a variant in MITF; alleles N/S; incomplete dominance in some breeds, recessive in others; N/S may show no white or some white depending on breed; S/S expected to show some white, sometimes nearly all white; other MITF mutations/white-spotting genes affect amount of white; test specific to the known MITF variant; French Bulldog listed as appropriate for testing; deafness not mentioned — Piebald/White Spotting (S Locus) — UC Davis Veterinary Genetics Laboratory, accessed 2026-11-03 — https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/piebald-dog
- Congenital deafness "associated with white pigmentation"; >100 breeds; "often associated with blue eyes and white pigmentation"; cochleosaccular deafness develops 1–3 weeks after birth, most common in piebald and merle dogs; unilateral may go undetected without BAER; BAER most definitive; unilaterally deaf dogs pass increased risk if bred; BAER + selective breeding best tools; DNA tests only for some forms — Deafness in Animals (Scheifele) — Merck Veterinary Manual, full review Aug 2025, updated Apr 2026 — https://www.merckvetmanual.com/ear-disorders/deafness/deafness-in-animals
- Blue eyes (absence of iris pigment) common with pigment-associated deafness; blue-eyed dogs statistically more likely to be deaf; deafness from degeneration of stria vascularis linked to absence of melanocytes; Dalmatian US prevalence 8% bilateral, 22% unilateral — Genetics of Deafness in Dogs — LSU School of Veterinary Medicine (G. Strain program), © 2026 — https://artsci.lsu.edu/vetmed/deafness/genetics.php
- French Bulldog appears on list of dog breeds with reported congenital deafness — Dog Breeds with Reported Congenital Deafness — LSU SVM — https://artsci.lsu.edu/vetmed/deafness/breeds.php
- BAER: each ear tested separately; usually no sedation; about 10–15 minutes (no minimum age on page) — What is the BAER test? — LSU SVM — https://artsci.lsu.edu/vetmed/deafness/baerexpl.php
- AKC standard: eyes "dark in color"; lighter eyes acceptable in lighter-colored dogs (paraphrase via summary — check exact wording); acceptable colors "All brindle, fawn, white, brindle and white, and any color except..." DQ: solid black, mouse, liver, black and tan, black and white, white with black; "Black means black without a trace of brindle"; nose other than black DQ except lighter colored dogs — Official Standard of the French Bulldog — AKC, approved June 10, 1991 — https://cdn.akc.org/FrenchBulldog.pdf
- FCI standard: eyes dark; "Light eyes (hawk eye)" severe fault; heterochrome eyes (wall eye) disqualifying; brindle with moderate or significant white spotting "so-called 'pied'"; fawn with white "fawn and white"; all-white with black eyelid edges and nose "admitted but not bred for, because of a risk of deafness"; deafness disqualifying; coat "without undercoat"; weight males 9–14 kg, females 8–13 kg — FCI-St. N° 101 Bouledogue français — FCI, 18.09.2023 — https://www.fci.be/nomenclature/standards/101g09-en.pdf
- RKC standard: correct colours brindle, fawn, pied; brindle pied / fawn pied = white predominates with patches; whites classified with pieds for show; eyes "preferably dark and matching"; coat "fine, smooth, lustrous, short and close"; ideal weight dogs 12.5 kg (28 lb), bitches 11 kg (24 lb) — French Bulldog Breed Standard — Royal Kennel Club, updated 1 Aug 2025 — https://www.royalkennelclub.com/breed-standards/utility/french-bulldog/
- RKC breed page: grooming "once a week"; coat short; sheds "Yes"; blue, cream etc. non-breed-standard; merle puppies not registered because merle "carries an increased risk of impaired hearing and sight problems"; "Health and temperament should always be a priority over colour" — French Bulldog breed page — Royal Kennel Club, accessed 2026-11-03 — https://www.royalkennelclub.com/search/breeds-a-to-z/breeds/utility/french-bulldog/

## Blue eyes
- "Blue and partially blue eyes are typically seen with merle"; double merles may have auditory, ophthalmologic, skeletal defects; French Bulldog appropriate for testing — Merle (M Locus) — UC Davis VGL — https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/merle
- 98.6-kb duplication upstream of ALX4 (chr 18) strongly associated with blue eyes, largely in Siberian Huskies, also non-merle Australian Shepherds; >3,000 dogs; dominant, highly but not completely penetrant; merle and piebald were the only previously known genetic causes — Deane-Coe PE et al., Direct-to-consumer DNA testing of 6,000 dogs reveals 98.6-kb duplication associated with blue eyes... — PLOS Genetics 2018;14(10):e1007648 — https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1007648
- Iris colobomas "most common in eyes that have more than one color in the iris" — Disorders of the Anterior Uvea in Dogs — Merck Vet Manual (owner), updated Sept 2024 — https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/eye-disorders-of-dogs/disorders-of-the-anterior-uvea-in-dogs
- Glaucoma signs: redness, corneal swelling and discoloration, enlarged eye, pain; "should be seen by a veterinarian immediately" — Glaucoma in Dogs — Merck Vet Manual (owner), updated Sept 2024 — https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/eye-disorders-of-dogs/glaucoma-in-dogs
- Dilute (MLPH, recessive): black → gray-blue, chocolate → pale silvery red ("isabella" in French Bulldogs); eye color not mentioned — Dilute (D Locus/Blue) — UC Davis VGL — https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/dilute-dog

## Flatulence
- Flatulence definition; most common cause diet change/dietary indiscretion; aerophagia esp. fast eaters; brachycephalic breeds (bulldogs, pugs) swallow more air due to breathing difficulty; soybeans, peas, beans, milk products, high-fat, spicy foods; fermentable fibers; "Most dogs and cats are lactose intolerant"; obesity and inactivity contribute; rule out IBD, SIBO, tumors, IBS, parasites, enteritis, EPI; with GI disease also loose stools, vomiting, weight loss; tests; highly digestible low-fiber low-fat diet; probiotics/prebiotics may help; small frequent meals for fast eaters — Flatulence in Dogs (Barnes, Hunter, Ward) — VCA / LifeLearn © 2024 — https://vcacanada.com/know-your-pet/flatulence-in-dogs
- Poncet 2005 abstract via Scilit: 73 brachycephalic dogs with upper respiratory syndrome; respiratory and digestive signs correlated, significant in French bulldogs; endoscopic lesions even without digestive signs; airway surgery may improve digestive signs — J Small Anim Pract 2005;46(6):273-279, doi:10.1111/j.1748-5827.2005.tb00320.x — https://scilit.com/publications/91c39cbdd8b841430f219556ea11c917 (details 49 Frenchies / 74% already in es/guias/SOURCES_VERIFIED.md)
- Bloat/GDV life-threatening emergency; signs restlessness, discomfort, rapid breathing, abdominal pain and swelling, repeated dry retching, drooling; mainly large deep-chested dogs — Disorders of the Stomach and Intestines in Dogs — Merck Vet Manual (owner), updated June 2026 — https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/digestive-disorders-of-dogs/disorders-of-the-stomach-and-intestines-in-dogs

## Shedding / hair loss
- "Dogs normally shed hair in the early spring and early fall"; also in response to changes in temperature or amount of sunlight; hair growth affected by nutrition, hormones, season — Structure of the Skin in Dogs (Moriello) — MSD/Merck Vet Manual (owner), updated Sept 2024 — https://www.msdvetmanual.com/dog-owners/skin-disorders-of-dogs/structure-of-the-skin-in-dogs
- Alopecia definition; shedding abnormal if obvious loss of coat and bald spots; if itching, investigate itch first; causes infections, parasites, allergies, hypothyroidism, protein deficiency, pregnancy/lactation, illness/fever, collar friction; diagnosis via scrapings, combing, cultures, biopsy, blood tests — Hair Loss (Alopecia) in Dogs (Moriello) — Merck Vet Manual (owner), updated May 2025 — https://www.merckvetmanual.com/en-ca/dog-owners/skin-disorders-of-dogs/hair-loss-alopecia-in-dogs
- Seasonal flank alopecia: French and English Bulldogs among predisposed breeds; well-demarcated symmetrical flank hair loss, skin often darker; usually begins in fall, regrows in spring; linked to day length; mainly north of 45° parallel; purely cosmetic; rarely never fully regrows — Seasonal Flank Alopecia (Hunter, Barnette) — VCA, updated 31 Jan 2024 — https://vcahospitals.com/apex/know-your-pet/seasonal-flank-alopecia

## Weight
- Up to 59% of dogs and cats overweight; 39% of owners of overweight pets thought weight acceptable; treats ≤10% of daily calories; obesity linked to high blood pressure, arthritis, diabetes, heart and respiratory disease, kidney dysfunction; losing weight too quickly can be dangerous — 9 Things You Should Know About AAHA's Weight Management Guidelines — AAHA, n.d. — https://www.aaha.org/resources/weight-management/
- Therapeutic weight-loss diets recommended for significant calorie restriction, fortified to avoid nutrient deficiencies; comorbidities incl. osteoarthritis, endocrine disease; rechecks 4–8 weeks apart reasonable for accountability program; preventing obesity may lengthen life span; failure to maintain weight loss is common — Cline MG et al., 2021 AAHA Nutrition and Weight Management Guidelines — JAAHA 2021;57(3):153-174 — https://www.aaha.org/wp-content/uploads/globalassets/02-guidelines/2021-nutrition-and-weight-management/resourcepdfs/new-2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines-with-ref.pdf ; https://www.aaha.org/resources/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/weight-reduction-in-the-obese-pet/
- Kealy lifetime study: 48 Labradors, littermate pairs, 25% fewer calories from 8 weeks; median lifespan 13 vs 11.2 years; restricted dogs needed osteoarthritis treatment about 3 years later (mean 13.3 y); funded/conducted by Nestlé Purina — Penn Today news release, 13 Sept 2002 — https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/study-finds-dogs-fed-reduced-calorie-diet-live-nearly-two-years-longer-dogs-unrestricted-diet
- Cambridge BOAS functional grade II "requires management, including weight loss and/or surgical intervention" — Recognition and Diagnosis — University of Cambridge BOAS Research Group — https://www.vet.cam.ac.uk/boas/about-boas/recognition-diagnosis
