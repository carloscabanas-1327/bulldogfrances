# Ledger es2: new facts verified 2026-11-03 (ES guides: papeles, revisión salud, ronca/respira, doble merle, diarrea, vomita)

- Spain, Ley 7/2023 art. 27 l): dogs, cats and ferrets may not be sold in pet shops; "Perros, gatos y hurones solo podrán venderse desde criadores registrados." Art. 27 m): prohibited to sell, donate or give up for adoption animals "no identificados y registrados previamente a nombre del transmitente". Art. 27 k): commercial breeding by breeders not in the Registro de Criadores is prohibited. — Ley 7/2023, de 28 de marzo, de protección de los derechos y el bienestar de los animales, BOE (texto consolidado) — https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2023-7936 . Caveat: the breeder registry depends on implementing regulation; do not state that it is fully operational.
- FCI has 101 members and contract partners (one per country) that "each issue their own pedigrees and train their own judges"; the FCI makes sure pedigrees and judges are "mutually recognised by all the FCI members". — FCI, Presentation of our organisation — https://fci.be/en/Presentation-of-our-organisation-4.html
- FCI standard No. 101 (French Bulldog), official valid standard published 10.08.2023: colour "fawn, brindled or not, with or without white spotting"; all-white dogs with black eyelid edges and nose "are admitted but not bred for, because of a risk of deafness"; weight males 9–14 kg, females 8–13 kg; disqualifying: black, black and tan "and all dilutions of black with or without white spotting", nose other than black, heterochrome eyes, "Completely closed nostrils", "Respiratory distress", "Deafness". Merle is not named and is not among the accepted colours. — FCI-Standard N° 101 Bouledogue français — https://www.fci.be/nomenclature/standards/101g09-en.pdf
- England: "No puppy aged under 8 weeks may be sold or permanently separated from its biological mother" and "A puppy may only be shown to a prospective purchaser if it is together with its biological mother" (licensed dog breeders). — The Animal Welfare (Licensing of Activities Involving Animals) (England) Regulations 2018, Schedule 6, para 1(5)-(6), legislation.gov.uk — https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/486/schedule/6/made?view=plain
- VCA "Vomiting in Dogs" (Barnes, Williams, Ward; updated 14 Aug 2026): vomiting involves abdominal contractions and effort, expelling "fluid, froth, or food"; regurgitation occurs quickly without abdominal contractions; see a vet immediately if vomiting with fever, lethargy, loss of appetite, abdominal pain, diarrhea, dehydration, blood in vomit, or suspected foreign body/toxin; if the dog seems well but vomits for more than 1–2 days, see a vet; home care per vet: short fast, water freely available, bland easily digested diet in small frequent portions, gradual return over several days. — https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/vomiting-in-dogs
- VCA "Diarrhea in Dogs" (Barnes, Llera, Williams, Ward; updated 3 Jul 2026): consult a vet if frequent liquid or semi-liquid stools persist more than two days; severe or bloody diarrhea or other signs of illness: seek attention as soon as possible; keep water available (dehydration can develop quickly); vet may advise brief fast or GI veterinary diet; return to regular diet gradually over about 7–10 days; check with vet before probiotics (quality varies). — https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/diarrhea-in-dogs
- Texas A&M CVM Cardiology Service, "Measuring Your Pet's Breathing Rate" (client info PDF): dogs and cats sleeping/resting normally breathe 15–30 breaths per minute; rates consistently above ~30 are abnormal; count 30 s × 2; contact the vet if consistently increased, emergency if with other signs. — https://vetmed.tamu.edu/hospital/wp-content/uploads/sites/15/2018/01/cardiology-home-breathing.pdf
- VCA "Food Allergies in Dogs" (Llera, Barnette, Ward; updated 24 Jan 2022): "the signs of a food allergy are usually itchy skin, paws, or ears or digestive disturbances such as vomiting or diarrhea." — https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/food-allergies-in-dogs . Note: the Merck owner page "Allergies in Dogs" does NOT mention GI signs of food allergy; do not cite Merck for that.
