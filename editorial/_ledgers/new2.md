## Lifespan
- UK life tables, 30,563 dogs that died 2016–2020: overall life expectancy at age 0 = 11.23 y (95% CI 11.19–11.27). French Bulldog 4.53 y (95% CI 4.14–5.01), shortest of 18 breeds; English Bulldog 7.39 y. Authors' caveats: small French Bulldog sample (n = 232), "recent soaring popularity", data "biased towards younger dogs". The Discussion says 4.55 y vs 4.53 in Results. Brachycephalic predispositions named: BOAS, spinal disease, dystocia (from other literature, not measured). — Teng KT, Brodbelt DC, Pegram C, Church DB, O'Neill DG. Life tables of annual life expectancy and mortality for companion dogs in the United Kingdom. Sci Rep 2022;12:6415 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9050668/
- UK, 584,734 dogs (284,734 deaths), 18 data sources: overall median 12.5 y; purebreds 12.7, crossbreds 12.0; brachycephalic purebreds 11.2 y vs mesocephalic 12.8 y (HR 1.4); French Bulldog median 9.8 y (HR 2.2, 95% CI 2.1–2.3), tied with Bulldog among the 10 breeds most at risk of early death. Caveats: cause of death not recorded; insurance data may under-represent brachycephalics; fast-growing breeds over-represent young dogs; UK only. — McMillan KM, Bielby J, Williams CL, Upjohn MM, Casey RA, Christley RM. Longevity of companion dog breeds: those at risk from early death. Sci Rep 2024;14:531 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-50458-w.pdf
- Dogs Trust/LJMU press release: flat-faced dogs "40% increased risk of living shorter lives"; O'Neill quote "flat-faced dogs live 1.5 years shorter lives". — Which dogs live longest?, LJMU news, 1 Feb 2024 — https://www.ljmu.ac.uk/about-us/news/articles/2024/2/1/dog-longevity
- VetCompass 2013 French Bulldogs: 98 deaths; 84 with recorded cause; median longevity 3.6 y; top grouped causes: brain disorder 11.9% (n=10), spinal cord disorder 9.5% (8), then lower respiratory tract 7.1%, mass lesion 7.1%, upper respiratory tract 7.1%, undesirable behaviour 6.0%, traumatic injury 6.0%, vertebral spinal disorder 6.0%. Population median age 1.3 y. "Longevity did not differ between males and females." Disorder prevalence: ophthalmological 10.5% (grouped), conjunctivitis 3.2%, prolapsed nictitans gland 2.6%, ulcerative keratitis 2.1%. — O'Neill DG, Baral L, Church DB, Brodbelt DC, Packer RMA. Demography and disorders of the French Bulldog population under primary veterinary care in the UK in 2013. Canine Genet Epidemiol 2018;5:3 — https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s40575-018-0057-9.pdf
- 50,787 neutered middle-aged dogs, 12 breeds, ~900 North American hospitals: "In all breeds, median life span was shorter in overweight compared with normal weight dogs"; Yorkshire Terrier 13.7 vs 16.2 y; German Shepherd 12.1 vs 12.5 y. — Salt C, Morris PJ, Wilson D, Lund EM, German AJ. Association between life span and body condition in neutered client-owned dogs. J Vet Intern Med 2019;33(1):89-99 — https://doaj.org/article/e600591be4cc47fb99570c82782bcf1c ; https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3029909/
- VetCompass 2021 (2,781 French Bulldogs; median age 1.51 y vs 4.48): corneal ulceration aOR 4.38 (2.95–6.50); ophthalmological grouped aOR 1.47 (1.25–1.73); spinal cord disorder aOR 3.00 (1.94–4.64); BOAS aOR 30.89; obesity aOR 0.30 (lower). — O'Neill DG et al. Canine Med Genet 2021;8:13 — https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s40575-021-00112-3.pdf

## Frenchton / Boston Terrier
- Boston Terrier BOAS: 104 dogs, 37.5% Grade 0 (vs 10% French Bulldogs, 15.2% Bulldogs in earlier data); Grade 1 35.6%, Grade 2 26.0%, Grade 3 0.96%; less prevalent and less severe. Boston Terrier is a "screw-tailed" breed. — Tomlinson F, O'Neill E, Liu NC, Sargan DR, Ladlow JF. BOAS in the Boston Terrier: A healthier screw-tailed breed? PLoS One 2024;19(12):e0315411 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11687697/
- DVL2 frameshift: strongest association with screw tail; fixed in Bulldogs and French Bulldogs; allele frequency 0.94 in Boston Terriers; segregates with thoracic and caudal vertebral malformations (recessive, incomplete penetrance for thoracic). — Mansour TA et al. (Bannasch DL). PLoS Genet 2018;14(12):e1007850 — https://escholarship.org/uc/item/95k394sh
- Designer crossbreeds (Cockapoo, Labradoodle, Cavapoo) vs parent breeds, 9,402 UK owners, 57 disorders: no difference in 86.6% of comparisons; 7.0% higher, 6.4% lower risk; Packer: do "not to assume their puppy will automatically be healthy due to being a crossbreed". — Bryson GT, O'Neill DG, Brand CL, Belshaw Z, Packer RMA. PLoS One 2024;19(8):e0306350; RVC press release 28 Aug 2024 — https://rvc-web-app01.live01.azure.rvc.ac.uk/vetcompass/news/new-rvc-research-debunks-belief-that-designer-crossbreed-dogs-are-healthier-than-purebreds?page=119
- "AKC Canine Partners is an enrollment program for mixed breeds." — AKC — https://www.akc.org/register/information/canine-partners/
- Boston Terrier standard (Royal Kennel Club, updated Nov 2025): weight classes under 6.8 kg (15 lb); 6.8–9.1 kg (15–20 lb); 9.1–11.4 kg (20–25 lb); eyes large, round, set wide; "Excessively prominent eyes" highly undesirable; tail short, straight or curled. — https://www.royalkennelclub.com/breed-standards/utility/boston-terrier
- AKC French Bulldog standard (approved 10 Jun 1991): tail "either straight or screwed (but not curly), short, hung low, thick root and fine tip"; weight not to exceed 28 lb (disqualification). — https://cdn.akc.org/FrenchBulldog.pdf

## Tail / spine
- CT study, 62 neurologically normal French Bulldogs: 93.5% had ≥1 hemivertebra (79.0% multiple; 243 total; T9/T10 most common); block vertebrae 6.5%, transitional 4.8%; 80.7% of all normal dogs (Frenchies, Pugs, Bulldogs) had ≥1 malformation; only one French Bulldog had a clinically relevant thoracic malformation; clinically relevant in 4.7% of Pugs. Retrospective; most normal dogs no neuro exam. — Ryan R, Gutierrez-Quintana R, ter Haar G, De Decker S. Vet J 2017;221:25-29 — https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/138321 ; https://rvc-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1393667
- Bertram et al. 2019 (149 CTs): in French Bulldogs screw-tail degree "correlates with the severity of hemivertebrae in the thoracic region"; LS hemivertebrae (L7/S1) associated with tail malformation and disc herniation; recommends minimising severe screw tails through breeding. — Broaddus KD, Clinician's Brief, Jan 2020 — https://www.cliniciansbrief.com/article/congenital-malformations-lumbosacral-vertebral-column
- RVC brachycephaly neurology page: hemivertebra = "half vertebra"; can cause kyphosis; common in clinically normal dogs; in French Bulldogs usually no signs but "should not be considered an innocent and benign condition": kyphosis changes gait slightly and raises thoracolumbar IVDD risk; IVDD "by far the most common spinal disorder in French bulldogs"; hard to separate relevant from incidental malformations on imaging; spinal arachnoid diverticulum predisposition. — https://www.rvc.ac.uk/research/focus/brachycephaly/health-issues/neurology
- RVC Clinical Connections (De Decker, Summer 2018): thoracic hemivertebrae very common in screw-tailed brachycephalics, mostly no signs; affected dogs can develop "progressive paresis and ataxia of the pelvic limbs", typically early in life; medical management poor outcome; surgical stabilisation good results. — https://www.rvc.ac.uk/clinical-connections/rvc-offers-new-treatment-for-congenital-vertebral-malformations
- Merck (dog owners; Packer RA, reviewed Feb 2018, updated Jul 2026): hemivertebrae, block and butterfly vertebrae; "Hemivertebrae are most common in screw-tailed dog breeds, such as Pugs, Bulldogs, and Boston Terriers"; CT may be needed to judge surgery. — https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/brain-spinal-cord-and-nerve-disorders-of-dogs/congenital-and-inherited-disorders-of-the-nervous-system-in-dogs
- Today's Veterinary Practice (Kerrigan & Robinson, 2016): most dysostoses radiographically apparent; MRI indicated with neurologic dysfunction; can occur without signs; prognosis variable. — https://todaysveterinarypractice.com/orthopedics/juvenile-orthopedic-disease-in-dogs-catspart-2-congenital-neonatal-orthopedic-diseases/
- 343 French Bulldogs with neurological disease (France): 222 myelopathies; Hansen I disc 70.3%; spinal arachnoid diverticulum 11.3% (n=25); compressive congenital vertebral malformation 8.6% (n=19), 17 of 19 hemivertebrae usually with major kyphosis; T6–T8, T10. — Mayousse V et al. BMC Vet Res 2017;13:212 — https://bmcvetres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12917-017-1132-2

## Eyes
- Dry eye (KCS): signs discharge, dull eye, redness, squinting, pigment/vessels on cornea; English Bulldog and Pug among predisposed; untreated → "potentially resulting in permanent vision loss"; "usually requires lifelong management". — Cornell Riney Canine Health Center, updated 2 Dec 2025 — https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/health-topics/canine-health-information/keratoconjunctivitis-sicca-kcs-dogs
- Proptosis: "sudden anterior displacement of the globe"; brachycephalic breeds predisposed; most commonly blunt trauma; "Approximately 25% of dogs recover vision". — Thomasy SM, Merck Vet Manual, Jul 2024 — https://merckvetmanual.com/emergency-medicine-and-critical-care/ophthalmic-emergencies-in-small-animals/proptosis-in-small-animals
