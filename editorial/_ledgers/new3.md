# Ledger new3: toxoplasmosis and dogs' eyes (EN + ES), 2026-11-18

Topic suggested by Carlos from a Mi Perro Fiel news item (https://www.miperrofiel.com/noticias/toxoplasma-ojos-perros-israel.html, 18 Nov 2026). Rewritten from scratch; every fact traced to the sources below (pages loaded with WebFetch on 2026-11-18).

## Study (Soueid et al. 2026)
- Bibliographic data: Soueid Y, Ofri R, Sebbag L, Aroch I, Pe'er O. More than a feline disease: ocular manifestations in five dogs infected with Toxoplasma gondii. Vet Parasitol Reg Stud Reports. 2026;75:101590. doi:10.1016/j.vprsr.2026.101590. Taken from dvm360's reference list + EurekAlert. The DOI resolves to ScienceDirect (pii S240593902600170X), which blocked automated access: abstract NOT read directly.
- EurekAlert release, The Hebrew University of Jerusalem, 8 Nov 2026 — https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1151003 — headline "A parasite linked to cats may also threaten dogs' eyesight, researchers find"; paper published 21 Sep 2026; Dr. Yamit Soueid + ophthalmology team, Prof. Itamar Aroch, Koret School of Veterinary Medicine; retrospective review of 5 dogs, Veterinary Teaching Hospital (Rehovot), 2023–2025; all 5 eye abnormalities + positive antibodies; 3 with fourfold rise; range anterior inflammation to retinal detachment and blindness; 6-year-old dog irreversible blindness + glaucoma, eye removed; dog with sudden bilateral blindness regained vision, lasting retinal changes; 8-year-old Weimaraner severe blepharitis, possibly first reported association; all improved initially on systemic clindamycin + ocular anti-inflammatory treatment; 2 recurrent inflammation and persistently high titers; several had underlying conditions/immunosuppression; small series cannot establish risk factors; positive antibodies alone cannot prove cause. Quote: "particularly when treating dogs with unexplained eye inflammation".
- dvm360, McCafferty C, 11 Nov 2026 — https://www.dvm360.com/view/toxoplasmosis-may-be-an-underrecognized-cause-of-ocular-inflammation-in-dogs — Jan 2023–Dec 2025; inclusion criteria (ocular disease, positive anti-T gondii IgG, ≥1 follow-up serology, ≥1 ophthalmic recheck); ages 3.5–8 y; 4 mixed breed, 1 Weimaraner; 2 bilateral anterior uveitis; 2 unilateral panuveitis with retinal detachment and blindness; 1 bilateral panuveitis with partial RD and acute blindness; 1 enucleation for secondary glaucoma; IFAT IgG 1:256–1:1024 (cutoff ≥1:64); comorbidities: prednisone for IMT, visceral leishmaniasis, atopic dermatitis with oclacitinib, later hepatic/splenic masses; Weimaraner relapse ~3 months after clindamycin with blepharitis/keratoconjunctivitis; all got oral clindamycin + topical prednisolone acetate; vision regained within 2 weeks; 2 relapses; TMS and atovaquone stopped (hypersensitivity, anorexia); IgM not available locally; suggested aqueous humor PCR, Goldmann-Witmer coefficient, Schirmer strip PCR; limitations: small, retrospective, IgG titers + clinical response.
- Discrepancy noted: EurekAlert says hospital in Rehovot, dvm360 says Jerusalem. Article avoids naming the city of the hospital.

## Background
- Merck Vet Manual (Dog Owners), Roman N, Jun 2026 — https://www.merckvetmanual.com/dog-owners/disorders-affecting-multiple-body-systems-of-dogs/toxoplasmosis-in-dogs — cats only hosts where life cycle completes, main source for other animals and people; healthy adults usually no signs; puppies and immunocompromised adults at high risk; prevention: wash hands/utensils/surfaces after raw meat, cook meat to ≥152.6 °F (67 °C); cats fed cooked/commercial food, litter cleaned daily.
- Merck Vet Manual (professional), Moré GA, May 2026, updated Sep 2026 — https://www.merckvetmanual.com/infectious-diseases/toxoplasmosis/toxoplasmosis-in-animals — felids only definitive hosts, oocysts in feces; infection via undercooked meat with tissue cysts (carnivores) or food/water with oocysts (all warm-blooded animals); generally subclinical in immunocompetent.
- CDC, About Toxoplasmosis, 11 Mar 2025 — https://www.cdc.gov/toxoplasmosis/about/index.html — freeze meat several days at sub-zero (0 °F) temperatures before cooking; change litter box daily.
