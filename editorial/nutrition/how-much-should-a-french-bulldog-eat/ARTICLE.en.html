How much should a French Bulldog eat? There is no single number. Here is how to find the right amount for your dog and keep it at a healthy weight.

Key takeaways

  • The feeding guide on the bag is a starting point. Adjust it to your dog's body condition.
  • An ideal body condition is 4 to 5 on the WSAVA 9-point scale: ribs easy to feel, a visible waist, and a tucked belly.[1]
  • Extra weight raises the risk of breathing problems in flat-faced dogs.[2]
  • Weigh food with a kitchen scale and count every treat.

Why there is no universal portion

Calorie needs depend on your dog's ideal weight, age, activity level, whether it is spayed or neutered, and its health. Two Frenchies of the same weight can need noticeably different amounts. The AKC breed standard caps adult weight at 28 pounds, but many healthy Frenchies are lighter.[3]

Check the label for calories. The World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) lists "how many calories per gram or serving of food" as one of the key things a label should tell you.[4] Foods vary widely, so switching brands without adjusting the amount is a common cause of weight gain.

How to judge body condition

Vets use a 9-point body condition score. WSAVA describes the ideal range (4 to 5) as: "Ribs palpable without excess fat covering. Waist observed behind ribs when viewed from above. Abdomen tucked up when viewed from side."[1]

  • Feel the ribs: run your hands along the chest. You should feel ribs easily under a thin layer, like the back of your hand.
  • Look from above: there should be a waist behind the ribs. Frenchies are stocky, but they should not look like a barrel.
  • Look from the side: the belly should rise up behind the ribs.

Check every 2 to 4 weeks, and weigh your dog monthly. For a small dog, even a pound or two is a meaningful change.

Why weight matters more in French Bulldogs

In a study of 854 dogs, researchers found that obesity "was found to further increase BOAS risk," on top of the risk from a short muzzle.[2] Extra fat around the neck and chest makes a narrow airway work even harder, and also adds strain on the spine and joints. Keeping a Frenchie lean is one of the simplest things you can do for its breathing.

A practical feeding routine

  1. Start with the amount on the label for your dog's ideal weight, not its current weight.
  2. Split it into two meals a day (three or more for young puppies, as your vet advises).
  3. Weigh the food in grams with a kitchen scale. Cups are surprisingly inaccurate.
  4. Subtract treats and chews from the daily total.
  5. Recheck body condition after 2 to 4 weeks and adjust by about 10% if needed.

Puppies, seniors and special cases

  • Puppies need a food labeled complete and balanced for growth. AAFCO says "complete" means all required nutrients are present and "balanced" means they are in the correct ratios.[5] Do not restrict a puppy's food without veterinary advice.
  • Seniors often need fewer calories as they slow down.
  • After spaying or neutering, many dogs need less food.
  • Dogs with medical conditions should have a diet plan from their vet.

When to call your vet

Call your vet if your dog gains or loses weight without explanation, is always hungry, or has ongoing vomiting or diarrhea. For a dog that needs to lose weight, ask for a target weight and a calorie plan rather than simply cutting meals.
