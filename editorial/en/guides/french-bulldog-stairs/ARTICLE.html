French Bulldog stairs are not automatically dangerous for a healthy adult dog: calm, supervised use may be possible if your dog moves comfortably. There is no breed-specific evidence that normal stair climbing causes intervertebral disc disease (IVDD). Puppies, dogs with pain or poor balance, and Frenchies recovering from spinal disease need more cautious advice from their veterinarian.

Key takeaways

  • French Bulldogs are one of the breeds most affected by intervertebral disc disease (IVDD). [1] Among 343 French Bulldogs referred with confirmed neurological diagnoses, 45.5% had disc herniation; this referral sample does not measure how common IVDD is in all French Bulldogs.[2]
  • French Bulldogs have inherited susceptibility to early disc degeneration; a causal effect of normal stair use has not been established.[1]
  • No controlled breed-specific study in this audit established that stairs cause IVDD. That does not mean every staircase is safe for every dog.[3]
  • Falls and high-impact jumping can cause injury.[4] A stable nonslip ramp and healthy body condition can be practical adjustments, but have not been proved to prevent IVDD.[3]

Why French Bulldog stairs are a question at all

French Bulldogs are a chondrodystrophic breed, which means their spinal discs age early. The Merck Veterinary Manual lists them among the breeds most often affected by disc disease, alongside Dachshunds, Beagles and Shih Tzus. That degeneration starts in the first months of life, and a disc can herniate as early as 1 to 2 years of age.[1]

The numbers in Frenchies are striking. In a retrospective French referral study, 156 of 343 French Bulldogs with a definitive neurological diagnosis (45.5%) had Hansen type I disc herniation. The median age was about 4 years, and around 40% of affected disc sites were in the neck. These percentages describe selected neurological cases, not population prevalence.[2] Separately, a retrospective follow-up of 127 French Bulldogs treated surgically for IVDD found recurrence signs in 52.7%. This does not predict the outcome of a healthy dog using stairs.[5] That is why owners worry about every step and jump.

Do stairs cause IVDD? What the evidence says

A causal link between everyday stair use and IVDD in French Bulldogs has not been demonstrated. The lifestyle evidence is largely from Dachshunds and cannot be extrapolated to French Bulldogs:

  • A 2015 UK Dachshund questionnaire linked lower activity and furniture restrictions with a history of IVDD. The authors noted possible reverse causation: owners may have changed activity after a diagnosis. Such associations do not show that jumping prevents disc disease.[6]
  • Dachshund Health UK, a breed health group, found daily stair use was linked with lower odds of IVDD in its survey. It still warns that jumping down from heights puts sudden stress on the spine.[4]

These observational surveys cannot prove cause or prevention. For an adult Frenchie that moves comfortably, calm climbing is not a demonstrated cause of IVDD. Prevent slipping, racing and falls, and seek individual advice after a disc episode.[3] If your Frenchie is carrying extra pounds, see our guide to the overweight French Bulldog.

French Bulldog puppies and stairs

There is no breed-specific rule for when a Frenchie puppy can start using stairs. The study most often quoted followed 501 puppies of four large breeds (Newfoundland, Labrador Retriever, Leonberger and Irish Wolfhound) in Norway. Puppies that used stairs before 3 months of age had a higher risk of hip dysplasia, while off-leash play on soft ground was protective.[7] It was not a study of French Bulldogs or of disc disease, so treat it as a reason for caution rather than proof.

The Royal Kennel Club also lists stairs and steep drops as a hazard for a puppy's developing bones.[8] In practice:

  • Supervise puppies on stairs; carry them when the steps are too high or slippery, and block unsupervised access with a safety gate.
  • Put a baby gate at the top and bottom of the staircase.
  • Let your puppy play on rugs, grass or other non-slip surfaces.

Going down stairs, jumping and falls

Jumping or falling from a height may cause injury, but the extra risk of routine stairs has not been quantified in French Bulldogs.[4] Do not use age thresholds from Dachshund guidance as if they had been validated in French Bulldogs.[4]

  • Use a leash or harness on stairs so your dog goes slowly.
  • Add stair treads or a runner so paws do not slip.
  • After a fall, if your dog seems painful, weak or unsteady, minimize movement and contact a veterinarian urgently.
  • A dog that suddenly avoids stairs may be in pain, or may be losing vision. Hesitating on stairs can be an early sign of vision loss.

Dog stairs or a ramp for the bed and couch

  • Pet stairs or a stable ramp may help some dogs avoid high furniture jumps; choose nonslip, secure equipment.[4]
  • Lift correctly: one hand under the chest and one under the rear, so the back stays level.
  • Harness, not collar: VCA advises a harness instead of a neck collar for all dogs with neck disc disease.[3] Pulling against a collar also raises pressure inside the eyes, which a harness avoids.[9]
  • After a disc episode or surgery: Dachshund Health UK notes that dogs that have already had a disc problem are at higher risk, so be extra careful with jumps and stairs.[4] Follow your vet's recovery plan.

Older Frenchies benefit most from these changes; see our senior French Bulldog care guide.

Back pain warning signs

Contact a veterinarian promptly for new back pain or reluctance to move. Emergency immediately: sudden inability to walk, dragging the back legs, loss of bladder control or rapidly worsening weakness. Do not make the dog climb stairs as a test. Minimize movement and seek veterinary care now.[1] For more detail, read our guide to French Bulldog back pain and weak legs.
