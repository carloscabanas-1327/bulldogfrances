Can French Bulldogs go up and down stairs? Yes, a healthy adult Frenchie can usually handle stairs at a calm pace. The concern with French Bulldog stairs is real but often overstated: the breed is prone to slipped discs, mostly for genetic reasons, so it makes sense to limit stairs and jumping for puppies, seniors and any dog that has had back trouble.

Key takeaways

  • French Bulldogs are one of the breeds most affected by intervertebral disc disease (IVDD). [1] In 343 Frenchies seen for neurological problems, almost half had a herniated disc.[2]
  • The main cause is early, genetically driven disc degeneration, not stairs.[1]
  • We found no study showing that stairs cause IVDD in French Bulldogs. VCA says there is no way to prevent the disease.[3]
  • Jumping down from heights puts sudden stress on the spine.[4] Fewer jumps (a ramp helps) and a healthy weight are sensible steps, since obesity is a risk factor.[3]

Why French Bulldog stairs are a question at all

French Bulldogs are a chondrodystrophic breed, which means their spinal discs age early. The Merck Veterinary Manual lists them among the breeds most often affected by disc disease, alongside Dachshunds, Beagles and Shih Tzus. That degeneration starts in the first months of life, and a disc can herniate as early as 1 to 2 years of age.[1]

The numbers in Frenchies are striking. In a French veterinary school study of 343 French Bulldogs with neurological disease, 45.5% had a Hansen type I disc herniation. The median age was about 4 years, and around 40% of the herniations were in the neck.[2] In a follow-up study of Frenchies that had disc surgery, more than half later had another episode.[5] That is why owners worry about every step and jump.

Do stairs cause IVDD? What the evidence says

No study shows that stairs cause disc disease in French Bulldogs. The research that exists comes mostly from Dachshunds, and it does not point the way many people assume:

  • An earlier UK survey of Dachshund owners (DachsLife 2015), summarized in a later study, found that dogs that were not allowed to jump on furniture, and dogs exercised less than 30 minutes a day, actually had higher odds of disc herniation. The later study's authors think this is at least partly because owners changed habits after a diagnosis.[6]
  • Dachshund Health UK, a breed health group, found daily stair use was linked with lower odds of IVDD in its survey. It still warns that jumping down from heights puts sudden stress on the spine.[4]

These are surveys, so they cannot prove cause and effect either way. The practical takeaway: for a healthy adult, walking up a flight of stairs calmly is not a proven danger. What is worth reducing is impact (leaping off the couch, racing down the stairs) and extra weight, because obesity is a known risk factor.[3] If your Frenchie is carrying extra pounds, see our guide to the overweight French Bulldog.

French Bulldog puppies and stairs

There is no breed-specific rule for when a Frenchie puppy can start using stairs. The study most often quoted followed 501 puppies of four large breeds (Newfoundland, Labrador Retriever, Leonberger and Irish Wolfhound) in Norway. Puppies that used stairs before 3 months of age had a higher risk of hip dysplasia, while off-leash play on soft ground was protective.[7] It was not a study of French Bulldogs or of disc disease, so treat it as a reason for caution rather than proof.

The Royal Kennel Club also lists stairs and steep drops as a hazard for a puppy's developing bones.[8] In practice:

  • Carry your puppy up and down stairs for the first few months.
  • Put a baby gate at the top and bottom of the staircase.
  • Let your puppy play on rugs, grass or other non-slip surfaces.

More on the first months in our French Bulldog puppy care guide.

Going down stairs, jumping and falls

Jumping down from a height is what puts the most sudden stress on the spine.[4] Dachshund Health UK also notes that puppies up to 12 months and dogs over 7 years are at greater risk of injury.[4]

  • Use a leash or harness on stairs so your dog goes slowly.
  • Add stair treads or a runner so paws do not slip.
  • If your Frenchie falls down the stairs, watch closely for the warning signs below over the next hours and call your vet if anything seems off.
  • A dog that suddenly avoids stairs may be in pain, or may be losing vision. Hesitating on stairs can be an early sign of vision loss.

Dog stairs or a ramp for the bed and couch

  • Pet stairs or a ramp for the bed and couch mean fewer jumps down, which is the movement that puts sudden stress on the spine.[4]
  • Lift correctly: one hand under the chest and one under the rear, so the back stays level.
  • Harness, not collar: VCA advises a harness instead of a neck collar for all dogs with neck disc disease.[3] Pulling against a collar also raises pressure inside the eyes, which a harness avoids.[9]
  • After a disc episode or surgery: Dachshund Health UK notes that dogs that have already had a disc problem are at higher risk, so be extra careful with jumps and stairs.[4] Follow your vet's recovery plan.

Older Frenchies benefit most from these changes; see our senior French Bulldog care guide.

Back pain warning signs

Go to a vet the same day, or an emergency clinic if it is after hours, if your French Bulldog cries out when picked up, refuses stairs or jumps it used to take easily, walks wobbly, drags or knuckles the back legs, or loses bladder control. Merck explains that surgical recovery in small dogs that cannot walk but still have deep pain sensation is 85% to 95%; once deep pain sensation is lost, it drops to about 50% or lower.[1] Hours matter.
