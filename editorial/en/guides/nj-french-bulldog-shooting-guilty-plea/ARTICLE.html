A New Jersey man has admitted in court that he shot and killed a young French Bulldog that had been left with him for safekeeping.[1][2] The case only came to light because a state laboratory examined the dog's body. It is hard news to read, but it raises a question every Frenchie owner faces before a trip: who exactly is watching the dog, and how would you know if something went wrong?

Key takeaways

  • Jeffrey Lentz, 57, pleaded guilty on November 16, 2026, to animal cruelty and possession of a weapon for an unlawful purpose.[1][2]
  • The victim was Ruby, a 1-year-old French Bulldog that her owner, his stepdaughter, had left in his care.[2]
  • A necropsy found a gunshot wound to the face, and an X-ray showed a bullet in the jawbone.[1][2]
  • He has not been sentenced yet. Sentencing is set for January 22, 2027, and prosecutors will ask for five years in state prison.[1][2]
  • Prosecutors will also ask the court to ban him from owning animals for life.[1]

The case in brief

On October 3, 2025, at about 9:05 p.m., Berkeley Township police in Ocean County, New Jersey, answered a call about an injured animal at a home in the township.[1] Investigators learned that the dog's owner had left her French Bulldog with Lentz, a Toms River resident, and later got a call saying the dog had been hurt and had died.[1] Local 12 identifies the dog as Ruby, 1 year old, and the owner as Lentz's stepdaughter.[2]

Lentz was arrested at his home on November 3, 2025, taken to the Ocean County Jail, and then released under New Jersey's bail reform laws.[1] On Monday, November 16, 2026, he pleaded guilty before Superior Court Judge Kimarie Rahill.[1] Ocean County Prosecutor Bradley D. Billhimer announced the plea the next day.[1]

How a necropsy revealed the truth

A necropsy is the animal version of an autopsy: a veterinary examination after death to find out what happened. Ruby's remains went to the New Jersey Animal Health Diagnostic Laboratory in Trenton.[1] The exam found that the wound to her face came from a gunshot, and an X-ray located a bullet lodged in her jawbone.[1][2]

The lesson for owners is simple. If a pet dies suddenly while in someone else's care and the explanation does not add up, you can ask your veterinarian about a necropsy before the body is cremated or buried. Without one, this case might never have reached a courtroom.

What happens next in court

A guilty plea is not a sentence. The judge will decide the punishment on January 22, 2027.[1][2] According to the prosecutor's announcement, the state will ask for:[1]

  • five years in state prison on the weapons charge, with one year before he can be considered for parole;
  • a three-year term for animal cruelty, served at the same time (concurrently);
  • a lifetime ban on owning animals.

These are requests, not decisions. Assistant Prosecutor Eleni Demestihas is handling the case.[1]

Choosing who watches your Frenchie

In this case the sitter was a family member, which is exactly the kind of arrangement most of us trust without a second thought.[2] Cases like this are rare, and nothing here suggests you should stop leaving your dog with relatives. But a few habits protect your dog whoever is in charge:

  • Hand over a care sheet. Write down feeding times, medications, your vet's number and the nearest emergency clinic. French Bulldogs overheat and struggle to breathe faster than many breeds, so add "no long walks in heat, call the vet if breathing gets noisy or labored."
  • Agree on check-ins. A daily photo or short video is normal and easy to ask for. A sitter who resists any update is a red flag.
  • Set the emergency rule in advance. Tell the sitter to call you and the vet at once if the dog is hurt or sick, and give written permission for emergency treatment.
  • Do a trial run. Before a long trip, try one night or one weekend first. Our guide on how long a French Bulldog can be left alone helps you judge how much company your dog needs.
  • Know your options if something seems wrong. If your dog comes back injured or dies and the story is unclear, talk to your veterinarian, keep any records and messages, and contact local police or animal control.

Boarding kennels and daycares bring their own questions, such as vaccine rules and how sick dogs are kept apart; see our guide to kennel cough in dogs before booking a stay.

What the reports do not say

The reports we read do not give a motive for the shooting, and we will not guess at one.[1][2] They also do not say what sentence the judge is likely to impose. We will update this article only if a court record or a new official announcement gives the outcome.
