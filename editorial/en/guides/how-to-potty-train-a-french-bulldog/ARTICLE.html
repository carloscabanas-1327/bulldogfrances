To potty train a French Bulldog, offer frequent bathroom breaks, reward success immediately and avoid punishment. Each puppy's schedule needs adjusting as it grows.

Key takeaways

  • Take your puppy out right after waking, after meals, after play, and at least every hour or two.[1]
  • The "one hour per month of age" rule is only a rough estimate of bladder capacity, not a safe interval for every puppy or a reason to delay breaks.[2]
  • Never scold an accident after the fact or rub your puppy's nose in it. It teaches hiding, not house training.[3]
  • Clean with an enzymatic cleaner, not ammonia or bleach.[4][5]

How often to take your puppy out

Young puppies don't give much warning, so you have to get there first. The Royal Kennel Club says that after a meal, puppies usually pee within about 15 minutes and poop within about 30, and that they need to go out "at least every hour or two".[1] The key times are:

  • First thing in the morning and last thing at night.
  • Right after every nap.
  • After every meal and after a big drink.
  • After play.[6]

Humane World for Animals (formerly the Humane Society of the United States) gives a handy rule of thumb: a puppy can hold its bladder for about one hour for every month of age.[2]

Puppy's ageLongest stretch between breaks, as a rough guide[2]
2 monthsAbout 2 hours
3 monthsAbout 3 hours
4 monthsAbout 4 hours

These age figures are rough estimates, not medically established maximums. Many puppies need more frequent trips, especially after waking, eating, drinking or playing; adjust the schedule to behavior and seek veterinary advice for unusually frequent or painful urination.

How to potty train a French Bulldog puppy, step by step

  1. Pick one spot in the yard or on your walk route, and take your puppy there on a leash each time.
  2. Use a cue word, like "go potty", just as your puppy starts. Over time it learns the link.[1]
  3. Reward right away, on the spot, with a small treat and praise. Waiting until you are back inside is too late.
  4. Supervise or limit space the rest of the time. Keep your puppy in sight, or in a crate or small pen when you can't watch.
  5. Keep a simple log of meals and potty trips for the first weeks. Patterns appear fast.

Apartment or not fully vaccinated yet? Discuss safe, low-exposure outdoor potty areas with your veterinarian. Pee pads can be a temporary indoor option, but changing the designated surface may slow some puppies' transition to outside. To move outdoors, shift the pad toward the door bit by bit, take your puppy out at the key times, and reward heavily for going outside. Until vaccines are complete, pick spots that unknown dogs don't use much. Our guide to distemper in dogs explains why.

On hot days, keep potty trips short and in the shade. Frenchies overheat quickly.

Using a crate to house train

A crate helps with house training. Humane World calls crate training "a reliable and humane method" of confinement.[2] Cornell's Riney Canine Health Center recommends introducing the crate with positive reinforcement and using a divider so a growing puppy doesn't have extra room to use as a bathroom.[7]

Ground rules from the RSPCA and Cornell:[7][8]

  • A crate must never be used as punishment.
  • Build up time slowly, starting with a few minutes.
  • Never crate a dog all day while you are at work, and don't force a distressed dog to stay in.

If long workdays are an issue, see can French Bulldogs be left alone.

Nighttime potty breaks

Many young puppies need one or more overnight breaks; individual needs depend on age, health and routine.[1] Humane World suggests removing water about two and a half hours before bedtime and notes that most puppies can sleep about 7 hours at night, while Blue Cross says many can't hold on all night "for several months".[2][4] Keep nighttime trips calm, quick and boring, with no play, so your puppy goes straight back to sleep.

Accidents: what to do and how to clean

Accidents are part of learning. If you notice your puppy starting to eliminate indoors, calmly guide it to the designated area without startling or punishing it.[3] If you find the mess later, just clean it up. The ASPCA says "a dog will not learn anything by being scolded for a past accident", and rubbing its nose in it can make your dog afraid of you and teach it to hide when it goes.[3]

How to clean:

  • Use an enzymatic cleaner that breaks down the odor, so your puppy isn't drawn back to the spot.[5][9]
  • Follow cleaner labels and never mix household chemicals. Bleach is not an ammonia-based product; avoid relying on strong scents instead of proper enzymatic cleaning,[4] and vinegar: strong chemical smells can encourage your puppy to mark the same spot again.[5]
  • Skip steam cleaners. Heat can permanently set the stain and smell.[5]

If a house-trained dog suddenly starts having accidents, see your vet. VCA notes that urinary infections, kidney disease, diabetes and other medical problems need to be ruled out.[9] Changes in poop matter too; our guide on how often a dog should poop explains what's normal.

How long does it take to potty train a French Bulldog?

Reliable house training can take weeks to months; different welfare and veterinary sources describe different milestones and training environments. The available sources do not support a breed-specific guaranteed schedule.[6][10][2] Plan for a few months with a Frenchie and celebrate progress.

If progress stalls: avoid labeling the puppy stubborn; first review opportunities to eliminate, consistency, stress and possible medical issues.[11] If progress stalls, go back to basics: more frequent trips, closer supervision, better treats delivered instantly, and a strict routine.
