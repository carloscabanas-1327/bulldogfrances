How to potty train a French Bulldog: take your puppy out on a tight schedule, reward the right spot every time, and never punish accidents. It works the same way as house training any puppy, but Frenchies are known for being a bit stubborn, so consistency matters even more. This guide gives you the schedule, the steps, and what to do when things go wrong.

Key takeaways

  • Take your puppy out right after waking, after meals, after play, and at least every hour or two.[1]
  • Rule of thumb: a puppy can hold it for about one hour per month of age.[2]
  • Never scold an accident after the fact or rub your puppy's nose in it. It teaches hiding, not house training.[3]
  • Clean with an enzymatic cleaner, not ammonia or bleach.[4][5]

How often to take your puppy out

Young puppies don't give much warning, so you have to get there first. The Royal Kennel Club says that after a meal, puppies usually pee within about 15 minutes and poop within about 30, and that they need to go out "at least every hour or two".[1] The key times are:

  • First thing in the morning and last thing at night.
  • Right after every nap.
  • After every meal and after a big drink.
  • After play or any excitement, like visitors arriving.[6]

Humane World for Animals (formerly the Humane Society of the United States) gives a handy rule of thumb: a puppy can hold its bladder for about one hour for every month of age.[2]

Puppy's ageLongest stretch between breaks, as a rough guide[2]
2 monthsAbout 2 hours
3 monthsAbout 3 hours
4 monthsAbout 4 hours

Treat these as upper limits, not targets. Every puppy is different, and the rule does not apply to the times right after eating, waking or playing.

How to potty train a French Bulldog puppy, step by step

  1. Pick one spot in the yard or on your walk route, and take your puppy there on a leash each time.
  2. Use a cue word, like "go potty", just as your puppy starts. Over time it learns the link.[1]
  3. Reward right away, on the spot, with a small treat and praise. Waiting until you are back inside is too late.
  4. Supervise or limit space the rest of the time. Keep your puppy in sight, or in a crate or small pen when you can't watch.
  5. Keep a simple log of meals and potty trips for the first weeks. Patterns appear fast.

Apartment or not fully vaccinated yet? Pee pads can work as a short-term step. To move outdoors, shift the pad toward the door bit by bit, take your puppy out at the key times, and reward heavily for going outside. Until vaccines are complete, pick spots that unknown dogs don't use much. Our guide to distemper in dogs explains why.

On hot days, keep potty trips short and in the shade. Frenchies overheat quickly.

Using a crate to house train

A crate helps because most dogs avoid soiling where they sleep. Humane World calls crate training "a reliable and humane method" of confinement.[2] Cornell's Riney Canine Health Center recommends introducing the crate with positive reinforcement and using a divider so a growing puppy doesn't have extra room to use as a bathroom.[7]

Ground rules from the RSPCA and Cornell:[7][8]

  • A crate must never be used as punishment.
  • Build up time slowly, starting with a few minutes.
  • Never crate a dog all day while you are at work, and don't force a distressed dog to stay in.

If long workdays are an issue, see can French Bulldogs be left alone.

Nighttime potty breaks

Very young puppies need at least one potty break during the night.[1] Humane World suggests removing water about two and a half hours before bedtime and notes that most puppies can sleep about 7 hours at night, while Blue Cross says many can't hold on all night "for several months".[2][4] Keep nighttime trips calm, quick and boring, with no play, so your puppy goes straight back to sleep.

Accidents: what to do and how to clean

Accidents are part of the process. If you catch your puppy in the act, interrupt calmly (a single clap is enough) and carry it to the right spot.[3] If you find the mess later, just clean it up. The ASPCA says "a dog will not learn anything by being scolded for a past accident", and rubbing its nose in it can make your dog afraid of you and teach it to hide when it goes.[3]

How to clean:

  • Use an enzymatic cleaner that breaks down the odor, so your puppy isn't drawn back to the spot.[5][9]
  • Avoid ammonia-based products, which smell like urine, as well as bleach and vinegar.[1][4][5]
  • Skip steam cleaners. Heat can permanently set the stain and smell.[5]

If a house-trained dog suddenly starts having accidents, see your vet. VCA notes that urinary infections, kidney disease, diabetes and other medical problems need to be ruled out.[9] Changes in poop matter too; our guide on how often a dog should poop explains what's normal.

How long does it take to potty train a French Bulldog?

Sources disagree, because every puppy is different. The Merck Veterinary Manual says house training "usually takes just a few weeks with patience and consistency". The PDSA says some puppies learn in a few days while others take much longer, and Humane World puts it at 4 to 6 months for a fully reliable dog.[6][10][2] Plan for a few months with a Frenchie and celebrate progress.

How to potty train a stubborn French Bulldog: the AKC says the breed can be stubborn and may need more training than others.[11] If progress stalls, go back to basics: more frequent trips, closer supervision, better treats delivered instantly, and a strict routine.
