Flying with a French Bulldog is possible, but your options are much narrower than with most breeds. Major US airlines, including American, Delta and Alaska, do not accept snub-nosed dogs in the cargo hold because of the breathing and heat risks, so the cabin is usually the only way. That means your Frenchie has to fit in a carrier under the seat. Here is what to check before you book.

Key takeaways

  • As of November 2026, American, Delta and Alaska/Hawaiian do not fly brachycephalic dogs as checked pets or cargo.[1][2][4]
  • In the cabin, US airlines limit carrier size (about 18 × 11 × 11 inches soft-sided) rather than weight. Many adult Frenchies are too big to fit comfortably.[1][3]
  • The AVMA advises a vet visit within 10 days of the flight and no tranquilizers.[6]
  • Policies change often; Alaska changed its rules twice in 2026. Confirm with the airline when you book.[4][5]

Can French Bulldogs fly in cargo?

Almost never, and that is for good reason. Short-nosed dogs have narrowed nostrils, a long soft palate and a narrower windpipe. They have trouble cooling themselves, and stress or heat can make their airway collapse. In the cargo hold, nobody is watching them.[6] The USDA warns that short-nosed dogs face a higher risk of heatstroke and breathing problems when exposed to stress or extreme heat.[7]

The record backs this up. US Department of Transportation data released in 2010 showed that about half of the 122 airline-related dog deaths over the previous five years involved short-faced breeds.[6] And a UK study of heatstroke in dogs found the French Bulldog among the breeds at significantly higher risk.[8] Larger US airlines must report every animal death, injury or loss in transport to the DOT.[9]

Flying with a French Bulldog in the cabin

For most Frenchies, the cabin is the only realistic option. Three things decide whether yours can go:

  • Size: American's soft-sided limit is 18 × 11 × 11 inches, and your dog must stay in the carrier under the seat in front of you for the whole flight.[1] The AKC standard allows French Bulldogs up to 28 lb, so many adults are a tight fit.[10] Your dog should be able to stand and turn around; a squashed, overheated Frenchie is at real risk.
  • Age: pets flying within the US must be at least 8 weeks old.[1][7]
  • Space on the flight: cabin pet spots can be limited, so add your dog to the booking when you buy your ticket.

Plan your luggage around the carrier: American lets you bring only one additional item on board with your pet.[1]

Flying internationally

Many international airlines set a cabin weight limit instead. As of November 2026, Lufthansa, KLM and Iberia all cap cabin pets at 8 kg (about 17.6 lb) including the carrier, and all three list bulldogs among the snub-nosed breeds they will not carry in the hold as checked pets. Each points owners to a separate cargo or air-freight service instead, which carries the same heat and breathing risks.[11][12][13] Since an adult French Bulldog typically weighs 18 to 31 lb (8 to 14 kg) under the FCI standard, most adults will not qualify.[14]

Coming back matters too. Since August 1, 2024, every dog entering the US, including your own returning dog, must look healthy, be at least 6 months old, be microchipped and have a CDC Dog Import Form receipt.[15] Dogs that have been in high-risk rabies countries face extra requirements, so check the CDC rules for every country on your trip.

Tips for flying with a French Bulldog

  1. See your vet within 10 days of the flight, and ask honestly whether your dog is fit to fly.[6] A Frenchie that snores loudly or gasps after mild exercise may not be; read our guide to French Bulldog noisy breathing.
  2. Keep a healthy weight. Extra pounds make breathing harder for short-nosed dogs.[6] See overweight French Bulldog.
  3. Get your dog used to the carrier weeks ahead, with treats and short sessions.[6]
  4. Do not sedate. The AVMA advises against tranquilizers for flying.[6] Talk to your vet before giving any medication.
  5. Avoid heat. Pick nonstop flights at cooler times of day. For checked and cargo pets, American says pets cannot travel when the current or forecast temperature is above 85°F anywhere on the itinerary, and it also restricts travel below 45°F.[1] Alaska may refuse pets when the forecast is above 85°F or below 45°F.[4]

Heat on the ground is just as dangerous: never leave your dog in a parked car, even for a few minutes. More in our guide to French Bulldog heat safety.

If your Frenchie is too big for the cabin

If your dog will not fit under the seat, first ask whether the trip is really necessary for them. A road trip with air conditioning and frequent stops, or staying home with someone they trust, is usually safer than any cargo workaround. If your dog is staying behind, our guide on whether French Bulldogs can be left alone will help you plan.
