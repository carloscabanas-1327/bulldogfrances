Do French Bulldogs shed? Yes. Their coat is short and smooth, but it is not hair that "doesn't fall out." French Bulldog shedding is usually light to moderate, with more hair around spring and fall. Normal shedding leaves the coat even; bald patches, redness or constant scratching are a skin problem, not shedding.

Key takeaways

  • The Royal Kennel Club lists the French Bulldog as a breed that sheds, with grooming "once a week."[1]
  • The FCI standard describes a smooth coat "without undercoat."[2]
  • Merck says dogs normally shed in early spring and early fall, and also with changes in temperature or daylight.[3]
  • Shedding is abnormal when it leaves obvious thin areas or bald spots. If your dog itches, the itch needs a vet first.[4]

Do French Bulldogs shed a lot?

Not compared with double-coated breeds. The breed standards all describe a short coat: AKC calls it "moderately fine, brilliant, short and smooth," and the FCI adds that it has no undercoat.[5][2] With no dense undercoat to "blow," Frenchies do not drop the clumps you see on a Husky.

They do shed steadily, though. The Royal Kennel Club's breed profile answers "Sheds: Yes."[1] The short, stiff hairs work their way into fabric and show up clearly on dark clothes and couches.

Two common myths:

  • "Short hair means hypoallergenic." The allergy academy AAAAI says no breed is truly hypoallergenic and that allergens come from dander, saliva and urine, not from hair length. See French Bulldogs and human allergies.[6]
  • "Fluffy Frenchies don't shed." Long-haired (fluffy) Frenchies carry a recessive coat-length gene; they still shed. Read more on fluffy French Bulldogs.

French Bulldog shedding season

The Merck Veterinary Manual notes that "dogs normally shed hair in the early spring and early fall," and may also shed in response to changes in temperature or the amount of daylight. Hair growth is affected by nutrition, hormones and season.[3]

Because temperature and daylight both play a role, indoor dogs living with heating, air conditioning and artificial light may shed more evenly through the year, including in winter, rather than in two clear seasons. Either pattern can be normal as long as the coat stays evenly covered.

Best brush and routine for French Bulldog shedding

A once-a-week grooming session, the Royal Kennel Club's guideline for the breed, is enough for most Frenchies.[1] Add a few extra sessions during heavier shedding.

  1. Rubber curry brush or grooming mitt. It lifts loose short hair and is gentle on thin skin. This is the most useful "French Bulldog shedding brush."
  2. Soft bristle brush to finish and spread natural oils.
  3. Damp cloth to wipe up the last hairs.

Go easy with metal de-shedding blades. A Frenchie has no undercoat for them to remove, and they can scratch the skin. While you brush, check the skin folds, tail pocket and belly for redness.

Bathing helps loosen hair, but too many baths can dry the skin and make things worse. Our guide on how often to bathe a French Bulldog has a schedule. A complete, balanced diet matters too: Merck lists protein deficiency among the causes of poor coat and hair loss.[4]

Living with a shedding Frenchie

Short, stiff Frenchie hairs weave into fabric, so a few habits help more than any single product:

  • Brush outdoors or on an easy-to-clean floor, and wipe your dog down with a damp cloth afterward.
  • Put a washable cover or blanket on your dog's favorite couch spot and wash it often.
  • Use a rubber squeegee, rubber glove or lint roller on upholstery and car seats; rubber grips short hairs better than a cloth.
  • Vacuum regularly with a pet-hair attachment, especially around beds and crates.

If anyone in your home has allergies, these steps reduce hair on surfaces, but they will not make the dog hypoallergenic, because allergens come from dander and saliva too.[6]

French Bulldog shedding excessively: common causes

If your Frenchie is suddenly shedding more than usual, Merck lists several non-skin triggers that can cause a temporary heavy shed: pregnancy and nursing, recovery from a serious illness or fever, and poor nutrition, especially low protein.[4]

The most common breed-related cause of hair loss, though, is itchy skin. In a large UK study, French Bulldogs had about 7.7 times the odds of allergic skin disease compared with other dogs.[7] Dogs that lick, chew or rub themselves break and pull out hair. Fleas are another classic cause; see fleas on dogs.

Bald patches that are not shedding

Merck says shedding becomes abnormal when it "results in obvious loss of the hair coat and bald spots."[4] In French Bulldogs, look out for:

Small patches around the eyes, lips or front legs in a young dogDemodectic mange. French Bulldogs had about 5 times the odds of juvenile-onset demodicosis in a UK study.[8] See demodectic mange.
Symmetrical bald flanks, darker skin, starting in fall and regrowing in springSeasonal flank alopecia. VCA lists French and English Bulldogs among predisposed breeds. It is "purely cosmetic."[9]

When to see a vet

Book a visit if you see bald spots, scabs, redness, flaking, a bad smell, constant scratching or licking, or hair loss with weight change or tiredness. Merck notes that when hair loss comes with itching, the cause of the itch should be investigated first. Diagnosis may involve skin scrapings, combing for parasites, cultures, a biopsy or blood tests.[4] Please do not treat with home remedies before you know the cause; many skin problems look alike.
