French Bulldog temperament is often affectionate and playful, but dogs differ in sociability, energy and training needs. Frenchies also have a stubborn streak, need a lot of company and handle heat poorly. Here is what the breed standards and research say about their personality, how they do with kids, cats and other dogs, and who they suit best.

Key takeaways

  • The AKC standard describes Frenchies as well behaved, adaptable and affectionate, with an even disposition: playful but "not unduly boisterous".[1]
  • In a large UK study, French Bulldogs had lower odds of recorded aggression than other dogs.[2]
  • They can be stubborn to train[3] and don't do well when left alone for long periods.[4]
  • They are generally good with kids, but no dog should be left unsupervised with young children.[3][5]

French Bulldog temperament and personality

The AKC's official breed standard says Frenchies are "well behaved, adaptable, and comfortable companions with an affectionate nature and even disposition; generally active, alert, and playful, but not unduly boisterous."[1] The international FCI standard sums up the temperament as "sociable, lively, playful, possessive, and keen companion dog".[6]

In everyday terms, owners can expect:

  • Affectionate: bred as companions, most Frenchies want to be close to their people.[4]
  • Quiet: the AKC says they "aren't a particularly vocal breed".[3] That, plus their small size and friendly nature, means they aren't natural guard dogs.
  • Stubborn: they "can be stubborn, so they may need more training than other breeds", according to the AKC.[3] Short, reward-based sessions work best. See how to train a French Bulldog.
  • Possessive: the FCI's word matters. Some Frenchies guard food, toys or a favorite person. If yours growls near its bowl, read our guide to food aggression in dogs.

Are French Bulldogs aggressive?

The data say they are less likely than average to be. In a Royal Veterinary College study of 2,781 French Bulldogs under UK veterinary care, the breed had lower odds of recorded aggression than other dogs (odds ratio 0.64).[2] That is a group average, not a promise about any one dog. Fear, pain, poor socialization and guarding can make any dog snap. If you are worried about your dog's behavior, see our guide to French Bulldog aggression.

French Bulldog temperament with kids

The AKC says French Bulldogs "are good around young children due to their size and lack of aggression".[3] The French Bull Dog Club of America adds a condition: they do well with kids "with proper early socialization. Both parties need to learn the rules!"[4]

Bite prevention applies to every breed. The AVMA says more than 4.5 million people are bitten by dogs each year in the US, and children account for at least half of bite injuries.[5] The CDC notes that most bites to young children happen during everyday activities with familiar dogs, including the family pet.[7] House rules that help:

  • Never leave a baby or young child alone with any dog, no matter how gentle.[8]
  • Teach kids to pet the back or shoulders, not the face, tail or ears.[5]
  • Leave the dog alone when it is eating, sleeping or caring for puppies.[5]
  • Learn the warning signs together: growling, going stiff or moving away.[5]

The AVMA also suggests that families consider waiting until children are older than 4 before adopting a dog, and teaching toddlers to be careful around and respectful of pets.[8] The CDC notes that nearly 1 in 5 people bitten by a dog needs medical attention, so supervision is worth the effort.[7]

One breed-specific rule: Frenchies shouldn't play hard for long, especially in warm weather. Teach children that the dog needs breaks to catch its breath.

With other dogs and cats

A well-socialized Frenchie usually gets along with other dogs and with cats, especially if they grow up together. Socialization in the first three months of life has the biggest effect, so introduce your puppy early to calm, vaccinated dogs and, if possible, a dog-friendly cat. Our guide to puppy socialization explains how. With adult dogs, introduce new pets slowly and feed them apart to avoid squabbles over food.

A few tips for first meetings:

  • Meet a new dog on neutral ground, both on leash, and keep the first meeting short.
  • Give a cat high places and escape routes the dog can't reach.
  • Reward calm behavior around the other pet, and separate them before anyone gets tense.
  • Pick up toys and chews at first, since some Frenchies are possessive.

Male vs female temperament

This is a common question, but we found no good research comparing the temperament of male and female French Bulldogs. Breed standards describe the same temperament for both sexes.[1][6] Differences between individual dogs, and how each one is raised, socialized and trained, are likely to matter far more than sex. If you are choosing a puppy, meet the parents and ask the breeder about each puppy's personality.

What a Frenchie needs to be happy

  • Company: the breed club says Frenchies "do not do well when left alone for extended periods of time".[4] The RSPCA advises leaving dogs alone no longer than about 4 hours.[9] More in can French Bulldogs be left alone.
  • Moderate exercise: the AKC says short walks or outdoor play each day should be enough, and that the breed is less tolerant of heat and strenuous exercise.[3]
  • Indoor living: the breed club says Frenchies are "not very tolerant of heat" and should be indoor dogs.[4]
  • Brain games: a toy stuffed with food gives mental stimulation and helps prevent boredom.[9]

Is a French Bulldog right for you?

The AKC says Frenchies can suit single adults, couples or families, and they have ranked as the AKC's most popular breed since 2022.[3][10] Are French Bulldogs good apartment dogs? The AKC calls the breed one of the world's most popular small dogs, "especially among city dwellers".[11] Their size, quiet nature and modest exercise needs suit apartment life, as long as someone is home a good part of the day and the apartment stays cool.

Be ready for the health side too. The same UK study found French Bulldogs had higher odds than other dogs for many common problems, including breathing, skin and ear disorders.[2] Before you buy, read our guide to French Bulldog breeder health tests.
