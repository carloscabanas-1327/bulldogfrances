If your Thanksgiving tradition includes turkey, the parade and a dog show, this year's National Dog Show is a milestone: its 25th edition, with more than 2,100 dogs entered.[1] For French Bulldog fans, the fun is spotting the Frenchie in its group and following it toward Best in Show. Here is when to watch, what is new this year, and where our breed fits in the lineup.

When and where to watch

The National Dog Show Presented by Purina was judged in mid-November and reaches TV on Thanksgiving Day, which this year is Thursday, Nov. 26. Coverage runs on NBC and streams on Peacock from 12 p.m. to 2 p.m. in every U.S. time zone, starting as soon as the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade ends.[2]

The familiar voices are back: David Frei and John O'Hurley co-host, and Mary Carillo returns as features reporter.[2]

A record 25th show

The Kennel Club of Philadelphia hosted this year's show at the Greater Philadelphia Expo Center in Oaks, Pennsylvania, its 25th year.[1] Organizers counted more than 2,100 competing dogs from over 200 breeds, and show chairman Frank DePaulo told FOX 29 it was the highest number of entries since 2017.[1]

Last year's Best in Show went to Soleil, a Belgian sheepdog.[1] Who follows her will only be known when the broadcast airs.

The three newcomers

Three breeds are stepping into the National Dog Show ring for the first time, each in a different group:[2]

  • Teddy Roosevelt terrier competes in the Terrier Group.[2]
  • Russian tsvetnaya bolonka joins the Toy Group.[2]
  • Basset fauve de Bretagne enters the Hound Group.[2]

None of them shares a group with the French Bulldog, so you won't see them in the same ring as the Frenchie during the group judging.

Where the Frenchie fits: the Non-Sporting Group

Each breed in the show competes within a group, and the broadcast builds up to Best in Show.[1][2] The French Bulldog is in the Non-Sporting Group.[3] The AKC describes that group as a diverse mix of breeds with varying sizes, coats, personalities and looks, and names "the compact French Bulldog" among its members.[3]

That variety is what makes the Non-Sporting judging fun to watch. The Frenchie shares the ring with breeds that look nothing like it. If you want to know what makes a Frenchie a Frenchie, our guides to French Bulldog history and French Bulldog temperament are a good start.

Watching tips for Frenchie fans

  • Set a reminder for noon. The show follows the parade directly, at 12 p.m. wherever you are in the U.S.[2]
  • Watch for the Non-Sporting Group. That is where the French Bulldog appears.[3]
  • Avoid spoilers. Results are revealed in the broadcast, so skip social media if you want the surprise.
  • Remember a show dog is a standard, not a shopping guide. A winning look on TV says nothing about a puppy's health. If the show makes you want a Frenchie, read our guide to health tests to ask a breeder for first.
