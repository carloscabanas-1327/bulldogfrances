The French Bulldog's history is a Paris story. According to the breed standard of the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI), the dog we know today took shape in the working-class neighborhoods of Paris in the 1880s, and France has kept the breed's official standard ever since.[1] This guide sticks to what the official breed documents say, and flags the popular stories they do not confirm.

Key takeaways

  • The FCI lists France as the breed's country of origin.[1]
  • The modern French Bulldog came from crossings made by breeders in the popular quarters of Paris in the 1880s.[1]
  • The first breed club was founded in Paris in 1880, the first registration dates from 1885, the first show appearance from 1887 and the first standard from 1898.[1]
  • In the United States, the French Bulldog was the American Kennel Club's most registered breed in 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.[3]

Where the French Bulldog comes from

The FCI's "brief historical summary" places the French Bulldog in the large family of molossers, the heavy-headed dogs of ancient Epirus and the Roman Empire. It names three relatives: the Bulldog of Great Britain, the Alaunts of the Middle Ages and the French mastiffs.[1] The FCI puts the breed in Group 9, among the small molossian-type companion dogs.[1]

Paris in the 1880s

According to the same summary, the French Bulldog as we know it is the result of crossings made by breeders in the popular quarters of Paris during the 1880s.[1] At first it was a dog of the city's market porters, butchers and coachmen. It quickly won over high society and the art world, and that is how it spread.[1]

How the bat ear became the rule

The upright "bat ear" is the French Bulldog's signature. The AKC standard describes it as broad at the base, elongated and rounded at the top, and states that any ear other than a bat ear is a disqualification.[2] The FCI standard also calls for erect ears.[1] Color rules have a history too: under the AKC's 2018 standard, colors outside the accepted list, such as merle and blue, are disqualifications. Our guide to double merle French Bulldogs explains why that matters for health.

From Paris to the most popular breed in the US

A breed born among Paris market workers is now the top breed in the United States. The AKC's ranking puts the French Bulldog at No. 1 for the fourth year in a row, covering 2022 through 2025. It was No. 2 in 2020 and 2021 and No. 4 from 2017 to 2019.[3]

Popularity has a cost. The French Bulldog Rescue Network warns that some breeders sell non-standard colors as "rare" or "designer" at inflated prices.[4] Before buying, read our guides to the health tests to ask a breeder for and the French Bulldog temperament.

Stories we have not been able to verify

Many websites say the breed began with toy bulldogs taken from England to France by lace workers, or tell of an American fight over bat ears versus rose ears around 1900. These stories are widely repeated, but the official breed documents we checked do not include them.[1][2] Following our editorial policy, we will add them only when we can cite a verifiable historical source.
