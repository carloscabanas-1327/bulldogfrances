Where should you buy a French Bulldog? The short answer: from a breeder who health-tests the parents and follows a code of ethics, or through a breed rescue. The French Bulldog is the most registered breed in the US,[5] so there are many sellers, and not all of them breed for health. This page explains where to look, what to ask and which offers to walk away from. We do not sell dogs or recommend specific breeders.

Key takeaways

  • The French Bull Dog Club of America (FBDCA) advises doing your homework first, visiting dog shows and talking to breeders on its referral list who follow its Code of Ethics.[1]
  • Ask for proof of the parents' health tests: hips, eyes, kneecaps and heart are required for the breed's CHIC number.[2]
  • "Rare" or "designer" colors such as blue, lilac or merle are outside the breed standard and are often sold at inflated prices.[3][4]
  • Adopting through a breed rescue is another option.[3]

Buying from a responsible breeder

The FBDCA says that buying a well-bred French Bulldog can be expensive and asks buyers to do their homework first. Its advice is to go to dog shows, talk with breeders who are breeding quality dogs, and contact breeders on the club's Breeder Referral lists, who have agreed to its Code of Ethics.[1]

The same brochure is honest about the breed: French Bulldogs weigh up to 28 pounds, live about 10 to 12 years, do not tolerate heat well and do poorly when left alone for long periods.[1] A good breeder will ask about your home and routine for the same reasons. Read our guide on whether French Bulldogs can be left alone before you commit.

Health tests to ask for

The FBDCA takes part in the Canine Health Information Center (CHIC). To earn a CHIC number, a French Bulldog must be microchipped or tattooed and have these results registered:[2]

  • Hips: OFA hip X-ray evaluation or PennHIP.
  • Eyes: a CAER eye exam by a board-certified veterinary ophthalmologist, ideally repeated every year.
  • Kneecaps: a veterinary patellar luxation evaluation.
  • Heart: a basic or advanced cardiac exam.

The club also recommends, without requiring, tests for juvenile hereditary cataracts (HSF4), cystinuria and other conditions, plus a breathing assessment.[2] Our guide to the health tests to ask a French Bulldog breeder for explains each one.

Rare colors: the biggest red flag

The French Bulldog Rescue Network (FBRN) calls colors such as blue, chocolate, lilac, merle and sable "fad colors," and says unscrupulous breeders market them as "rare" or "designer" to sell dogs at inflated prices.[3] Under the AKC's 2018 standard, merle and blue are disqualifications.[4] Some carry known health risks: FBRN cites color dilution alopecia in dilute dogs and, citing UC Davis, a higher chance of deafness and serious eye problems in double merles.[3] See our guide to double merle French Bulldogs.

Adopting a French Bulldog from a rescue

Breed rescues such as the French Bulldog Rescue Network, a volunteer-run nonprofit, take in French Bulldogs that need new homes, including surrendered dogs from fad-color breeding.[3] If you adopt, ask the rescue for the dog's known health history and any vet records.

Before you pay: a checklist

  • Can you see the health test results for both parents, and check them in the OFA database?[2]
  • Is the breeder on the FBDCA referral list or able to show they follow a code of ethics?[1]
  • Is the puppy's color within the breed standard?[4]
  • Do you get complete paperwork? See our French Bulldog puppy paperwork guide.
  • Will a vet check the puppy soon after pickup? Use our puppy health checklist.
