Is your French Bulldog too skinny, or does it just look that way? Start by checking body condition, because many owners see an ideal-weight dog as thin. If your Frenchie really is underweight, the usual suspects are worms, not enough calories, digestive problems and, in this breed, vomiting or regurgitation linked to breathing trouble. Here is how to tell, and what to do.

Key takeaways

  • Judge weight by body condition: ideal is 4 to 5 on a 9-point scale, with ribs easy to feel and a visible waist.[1]
  • Poor growth and loss of condition are often the first signs of roundworms in young puppies.[2]
  • A dog that eats a lot but keeps losing weight may not be absorbing food, for example because of exocrine pancreatic insufficiency.[3]
  • In one study, about 74% of flat-faced dogs with breathing problems had moderate or severe drooling, regurgitation or vomiting.[4]

Is my French Bulldog too skinny? How to check

Vets use a body condition score (BCS) from 1 to 9. The World Small Animal Veterinary Association (WSAVA) says the goal for most pets is 4 or 5, and notes that owners often think a pet at ideal weight looks too thin.[1]

  • Ideal: you can feel the ribs easily under a thin layer of fat, you see a waist from above, and the belly tucks up from the side.[1]
  • Too thin: ribs, spine and hip bones are visible from a distance, and there is little muscle.

Check muscle too. WSAVA recommends assessing muscle separately by feeling over the head, shoulder blades, spine and hips, because a dog can be a reasonable weight and still be losing muscle.[1] A very skinny French Bulldog with a bony head or spine, or one that is losing weight without explanation, should see a vet.

How much should a French Bulldog weigh?

The AKC standard says a French Bulldog should not exceed 28 lb.[5] The international FCI standard gives 20 to 31 lb (9 to 14 kg) for males and 18 to 29 lb (8 to 13 kg) for females.[6] Frame size varies a lot within that range, so two Frenchies of the same weight can be in very different condition. Body condition matters more than the number on the scale.

Keep in mind that in this breed, extra weight is usually the bigger health risk: it worsens breathing and sleep. Aim for ideal, not "chunky." See our guide to the overweight French Bulldog for the other side of the scale.

French Bulldog puppy not gaining weight

Weigh your puppy weekly on the same scale. The Merck Veterinary Manual says puppies gain roughly 2 to 4 grams a day for each kilogram of expected adult weight during the first 5 months, and growth starts to level off after 6 months.[7] For a Frenchie expected to reach about 25 lb (11 kg), that works out to roughly 0.8 to 1.6 oz a day, or about 5 to 11 oz a week. Growth charts developed by Waltham with the University of Liverpool are available, and your vet can use them to track your puppy.[8]

Common reasons a puppy falls behind:

  • Roundworms: puppies are most often infected through the mother before birth. Poor growth, a dull coat and a pot belly are early signs, which is why deworming starts at 2 weeks of age.[2]
  • Not enough food, or the wrong food: use a complete and balanced puppy food and check the portion. See how much a French Bulldog should eat.
  • Birth defects: a cleft palate (milk coming from the nose), megaesophagus (regurgitating once solid food starts) or a portosystemic liver shunt, the most common liver defect present at birth, which can cause "a failure to grow and thrive."[9]

In very young puppies, failing to gain weight is often the first sign of illness, and problems can get worse very quickly. Call your vet right away.[10]

Why is my French Bulldog so skinny when he eats?

If your dog eats eagerly but stays thin or loses weight, your vet will look at how well food is being digested and absorbed. Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI) causes weight loss despite a big appetite, with large, pale, foul-smelling stools. Signs appear only after about 90% or more of the digestive part of the pancreas is lost, and a blood test confirms it.[3] Other possible causes include intestinal parasites, inflammatory bowel disease, food allergies and hormone problems. Stool changes are a useful clue; see how often a dog should poop for what is normal.

Vomiting, regurgitation and breathing

In French Bulldogs, the airway and the digestive tract are linked. In a study of 73 flat-faced dogs referred for breathing problems (about two-thirds were French Bulldogs), around 74% had moderate or severe drooling, regurgitation or vomiting. In the French Bulldogs, the worse the breathing signs, the worse the digestive signs.[4]

If your Frenchie brings food back up after eating, drools heavily or gags, tell your vet. Treating the airway and any reflux can make a real difference to weight. Our guide to French Bulldog vomiting vs. regurgitation explains how to tell the two apart.

How to help your French Bulldog gain weight

  • Get a diagnosis first. Feeding more will not fix worms or a digestive disease.
  • Feed a complete, good-quality food split into several meals a day. Our guide to the best food for French Bulldogs explains what to look for.
  • Increase gradually: raise the portion a little at a time and weigh weekly.
  • Skip table scraps and improvised home cooking: they unbalance the diet, and some human foods are toxic to dogs.
  • Do not rush a puppy: Merck notes that overfeeding speeds growth, which is not desirable.[7]

Once your dog reaches a healthy weight, hold it there with regular body condition checks.

When to see the vet

  • Weight loss with no clear reason, or a puppy that stops gaining between weekly weigh-ins.
  • Diarrhea, vomiting, regurgitation or unusual stools.
  • A big appetite but continued weight loss.
  • Refusing food for more than a day, lethargy or drinking much more water than usual.
