Kennel cough in dogs is a contagious respiratory illness that often causes a harsh cough. Many mild cases improve within one to two weeks, but French Bulldogs with breathing difficulties need prompt veterinary attention.[7]

Key takeaways

  • Several germs cause it, mainly the bacterium Bordetella bronchiseptica, canine parainfluenza virus and canine adenovirus type 2.[1]
  • Mild cases usually clear in 7 to 10 days;[2] the full illness runs about 10 to 20 days.[1]
  • Short-nosed dogs with existing airway problems may struggle more when respiratory disease worsens. Ask a veterinarian promptly if breathing changes, rather than assuming every cough is harmless.[3][7]
  • Fever, lethargy, loss of appetite or labored breathing can mean pneumonia: see a vet the same day.[1][4]

Kennel cough symptoms and sound

The classic sign is a loud, dry cough that sounds like a goose honk, often followed by retching or gagging, as if the dog is trying to bring something up. Gentle pressure on the windpipe easily sets it off,[1] so a collar pulling on a walk can trigger a coughing fit. Some dogs also sneeze or have a runny nose.[2]

Signs typically appear 2 to 10 days after exposure (about 6 days on average for Bordetella).[2] So a cough that starts a few days after daycare, a grooming appointment or a boarding stay fits the pattern.

Practical tip for Frenchies: while your dog is coughing, walk on a harness, not a collar, to keep pressure off the trachea.

How long does kennel cough last?

In most dogs, severity drops during the first 5 days, and the illness lasts 10 to 20 days in total.[1] Many mild cases clear completely within 7 to 10 days.[2][3] Stress can bring the cough back during recovery.[1]

A cough that is still going strong after two weeks, or that gets worse instead of better, needs a recheck.[3]

Why French Bulldogs need closer watching

French Bulldogs can have brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome (BOAS), so coughing, fever and airway inflammation deserve particular attention in dogs that already breathe noisily or tire easily. The AVMA recommends veterinary assessment for respiratory symptoms and immediate attention for rapid or difficult breathing. These precautions reflect individual clinical condition, not a claim that every French Bulldog will develop severe infection.[7] Learn more in our guide to French Bulldog noisy breathing.

When kennel cough is an emergency

Kennel cough can turn into pneumonia, especially in puppies.[1] Call your vet the same day if you notice:

  • Fever, lethargy or refusing food[1]
  • Thick yellow or green nasal discharge[1]
  • A wet, productive cough[1][4]
  • Coughing so hard it causes vomiting[3]
  • A cough that lingers or worsens[3]

Go to an emergency vet now if breathing is labored or your dog is struggling to get air.[3][4]

Is kennel cough contagious?

To other dogs: very. It spreads through droplets from coughing and sneezing, direct contact, and shared objects like bowls and toys. It moves fastest where dogs are close together: daycare, boarding, shelters, groomers, dog parks and vet clinics.[1][2] Dogs can spread it while looking healthy.[3]

How long: the contagious period depends on the organism involved. Cornell reports that dogs infected with Bordetella bronchiseptica may remain contagious for up to eight weeks or longer even when the cough has stopped; this is not a universal timeline for every cause of kennel cough.[2] Keep your dog apart from other dogs and ask your veterinarian when group activities are safe again. Clean shared objects with a suitable disinfectant according to the label, without mixing cleaning products.

To cats and people: rarely. Bordetella bronchiseptica can also make cats sick, and it has infrequently caused illness in people, typically those with weakened immune systems.[2] If someone in your home is immunocompromised, mention it to your vet and your doctor.

Kennel cough treatment at home and at the vet

Most dogs with only a cough recover at home. According to the Merck Veterinary Manual, antibiotics are usually not needed unless pneumonia is present, and a vet may prescribe a cough suppressant for a persistent dry cough (never when there is pneumonia).[1] Cornell adds that steroids and anti-inflammatories do not shorten the illness.[4]

At home:

  • Rest, with short, calm potty walks on a harness.
  • Fresh water always available and a smoke-free home.
  • Keep your Frenchie cool; heat and excitement make breathing harder.
  • No human cough syrups. Some contain ingredients that are toxic to dogs, and dosing should only come from your vet.

Kennel cough vaccine

Bordetella vaccines come as intranasal (nose drops), oral and injectable forms. The 2022 AAHA Canine Vaccination Guidelines class them as noncore, meaning they are recommended based on lifestyle and risk. The intranasal or oral version is a single dose with yearly boosters; the injectable needs 2 doses 2 to 4 weeks apart, then yearly boosters.[5] Canine influenza vaccines are also noncore.[5] WSAVA calls respiratory vaccines non-core for pet dogs in typical homes but important in shelters.[6]

Cornell recommends the vaccine for dogs that board, visit the groomer or play at dog parks, and the same logic applies to daycare. A vaccinated dog can still catch kennel cough, but it usually gets milder disease and a lower risk of pneumonia.[4] Puppies are building their whole vaccine series at the same time, so see our guides to distemper in dogs and safe puppy socialization.
